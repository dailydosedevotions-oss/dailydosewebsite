Daily Dose #201

November 11, 2026

Work Produced by Faith

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on November 11, 2026.

1 THESSALONIANS 1:3 (ESV)

"Remembering before our God and Father your work of faith and labor of love..."

Faith is not meant to remain invisible forever.

Paul remembers the Thessalonian believers before God and speaks of their "work of faith" in 1 THESSALONIANS 1:3 (ESV). That phrase matters. Their work did not replace faith. It flowed from faith. True trust in Christ begins to shape the life.

This protects us from two errors.

One error says works save us. Scripture is clear that they do not. We are saved by grace through faith, not because of our works. But the other error says faith has no fruit. Scripture will not allow that either. Living faith bears evidence.

The Thessalonians had turned to God from idols. Their faith had direction. It affected worship, allegiance, endurance, and witness. They did not merely add Christian language to an unchanged life. The gospel came with power, and their lives began to show it.

JAMES 2:17 (ESV) says, "faith by itself, if it does not have works, is dead." That verse does not teach salvation by human merit. It teaches that genuine faith is not lifeless. A living root produces fruit.

This should lead us to honest examination, not panic. The question is not whether we can produce enough works to earn God's love. We cannot. The question is whether our professed faith is actually shaping how we live.

Does faith affect how you speak when irritated?

Does it affect what you do when no one sees?

Does it affect repentance, generosity, purity, forgiveness, patience, and the way you handle responsibility?

Faith works because faith receives and responds to Christ.

Paul also mentions their "labor of love." Love in Scripture is not only sentiment. It serves. It bears cost. It keeps showing up. It does not always feel convenient, but it acts for the good of another before God.

Then he speaks of "steadfastness of hope in our Lord Jesus Christ." Faith, love, and hope belong together. Hope in Christ helps believers endure when obedience becomes costly.

There is a quiet challenge here. If faith never moves our hands, our habits, our words, or our choices, we need to come honestly before the Lord. But there is also encouragement. Ordinary obedience done by faith matters to God.

The meal served in love. The apology offered in humility. The temptation resisted. The burden carried. The truth spoken gently. The work done faithfully. These may look small, but when they spring from faith in Christ, they are not empty.

Faith does not work to become loved.

Faith works because Christ has loved us first.
