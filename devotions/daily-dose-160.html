Daily Dose #160

October 1, 2026

SLOW TO SPEAK

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on October 1, 2026.

JAMES 1:19-20 (ESV)

"Let every person be quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger; for the anger of man does not produce the righteousness of God."

Our words often reveal what has been happening in the heart before anyone else could see it.

James gives a simple but searching command: be quick to hear, slow to speak, and slow to anger. It is simple enough to understand, but not always easy to live, especially in moments where we feel misunderstood, accused, pressured, ignored, or disrespected.

When emotions rise, speech often runs ahead of wisdom.

We answer before listening. We defend before understanding. We accuse before asking. We interrupt because we are already preparing our response while the other person is still speaking. Then, once words leave the mouth, they can do damage that apologies may not quickly repair.

James does not say, "Never speak." He says, "slow to speak."

There is a difference. The Bible does not call believers into silence when truth needs to be spoken. There are moments where silence would be cowardice or compromise. But Scripture does call us away from impulsive, angry, careless speech that is ruled by the flesh instead of the Spirit.

Human anger can feel powerful in the moment. It can feel like clarity. It can feel like strength. It can feel justified. But James says "the anger of man does not produce the righteousness of God."

That sentence needs to search us.

There may be times when something is truly wrong and needs to be addressed. There may be conversations that require courage. There may be moments where truth must be spoken plainly. But even then, anger cannot be allowed to become lord of the mouth.

Anger often tells us we have the right to speak however we want because the feeling is strong. But a strong feeling is not the same as a righteous response. The Lord cares not only that truth is spoken, but how it is carried.

PROVERBS 10:19 (ESV) says, "When words are many, transgression is not lacking, but whoever restrains his lips is prudent."

Sometimes wisdom sounds like fewer words.

Sometimes obedience looks like waiting before answering. Sometimes love looks like asking one more question instead of making one more accusation. Sometimes maturity looks like refusing to send the message while your heart is still heated.

Jesus said in MATTHEW 12:36 (ESV), "On the day of judgment people will give account for every careless word they speak."

That is sobering. Words are not weightless before God. The things we say in frustration, pride, sarcasm, bitterness, and carelessness matter. We may excuse them quickly, but Scripture does not treat speech lightly.

This does not mean we live in terror every time we speak. It means we bring our mouths under the lordship of Christ.

Before you answer, ask what is leading you. Is it love or pride? Is it truth or irritation? Are you trying to restore or trying to win? Are you speaking to build up, or to make someone feel the weight of your anger?

PROVERBS 18:21 (ESV) says, "Death and life are in the power of the tongue."

That verse is not exaggerating for effect. Words can bring life into a moment, and words can bring damage. They can help someone see clearly, or they can crush. They can open a door for peace, or they can stir up more conflict.

This is especially important in the places where we feel most familiar. Many people are careful with words in public but careless at home. They measure tone with strangers but release frustration on the people closest to them. Yet the call of James does not stop at the front door. The people nearest to us should not receive the least governed version of our speech.

Being quick to hear is also part of obedience.

It is not only about speaking less. It is about listening rightly. Sometimes we are slow to hear because we assume we already know. Sometimes we are slow to hear because pride is preparing a defence. Sometimes we are slow to hear because we care more about being right than understanding what is actually being said.

PROVERBS 18:13 (ESV) says, "If one gives an answer before he hears, it is his folly and shame."

That verse is direct. Answering before hearing is not wisdom. It may feel quick, sharp, and confident, but Scripture calls it folly. A surrendered person learns to listen before responding, because love is not in a hurry to misunderstand.

Being slow to speak gives room for the Spirit to work in the pause.

It gives room for prayer. It gives room for conviction. It gives room for wisdom to replace reaction. It gives room for the heart to ask, "Lord, what would honour You here?"

There is strength in being slow to speak. Not because words do not matter, but because they matter so much.

Do not hand your mouth over to the first emotion that rises.

Let the Lord rule the response.
