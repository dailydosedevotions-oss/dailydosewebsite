Daily Dose #155

September 26, 2026

A FAITH THAT LISTENS

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on September 26, 2026.

JAMES 1:22 (ESV)

"But be doers of the word, and not only hearers, deluding your own selves."

Listening to Scripture is a gift, but James tells us that hearing alone is not the full picture.

The Word of God is meant to lead us into obedience. If we listen but refuse to respond, we can begin to deceive ourselves. That is a serious warning because self-deception can still sound spiritual. It can quote truth, agree with truth, talk about truth, and still resist the very thing God is calling us to do.

James is not attacking people for hearing the Word. Hearing matters. Faith comes from hearing, and hearing through the word of Christ. The danger James confronts is hearing without surrender.

It is possible to enjoy a message but ignore conviction.

It is possible to know the right verses but avoid the step of obedience.

It is possible to say, "That was powerful," and then walk away unchanged because the heart never yielded.

Faith that listens also responds.

Obedience is not how we earn God's love. We are saved by grace, not by our works. But obedience is one of the ways faith becomes visible. When the Word exposes bitterness, we forgive. When it confronts pride, we humble ourselves. When it reveals sin, we repent. When it calls us to love, we act. When it teaches holiness, we do not treat compromise as harmless.

Jesus asks in LUKE 6:46 (ESV), "Why do you call me 'Lord, Lord,' and not do what I tell you?"

That question searches the heart. To call Jesus Lord while refusing His words is not a small contradiction. It reveals a divided place within us. We may want the comfort of belonging to Him without the surrender of obeying Him.

But Jesus is not merely a teacher to admire.

He is Lord.

That means His words carry authority over our preferences. His commands are not suggestions for moments when obedience feels convenient. His truth is not something we keep at a distance until it agrees with what we already wanted to do.

The Word of God is not given to decorate our thinking. It is given to renew the mind, expose sin, train righteousness, correct error, and lead us into the life God calls good.

2 TIMOTHY 3:16-17 (ESV) says, "All Scripture is breathed out by God and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for training in righteousness."

Scripture teaches, but it also reproves. It encourages, but it also corrects. It comforts, but it also trains. If we only receive the parts that soothe us and reject the parts that confront us, we are not truly letting the Word lead.

This is where humility matters.

A humble heart says, "Lord, show me where I need to obey." Pride says, "I already know." A humble heart allows Scripture to examine motives. Pride uses Scripture mainly to examine everyone else.

James later compares the Word to a mirror. A mirror shows what is there. But if a person looks into the mirror and then walks away without responding, the looking did not lead to change. The issue was not the mirror. The issue was the refusal to act on what was shown.

There are times Scripture shows us something clearly, and we try to move on quickly because obedience would cost us.

Maybe the Word has shown you that resentment has been growing. Maybe it has exposed compromise you keep minimising. Maybe it has called you to forgive, apologise, confess, serve, speak truth, change direction, or stop feeding something that is damaging your soul.

Do not rush past conviction.

Conviction is mercy when it leads us back to God.

JOHN 14:15 (ESV) records Jesus saying, "If you love me, you will keep my commandments."

Love for Christ is not proved by emotional language alone. It shows itself in obedience. Not perfect obedience, as though we no longer need grace, but real obedience, humble obedience, repentant obedience, obedience that takes His words seriously.

This is where the Christian life becomes very practical.

It is not only what we know about forgiveness, but whether we forgive. It is not only what we believe about humility, but whether we humble ourselves when pride rises. It is not only what we confess about holiness, but whether we turn from the sin Scripture exposes. It is not only agreeing that prayer matters, but actually returning to the Lord in prayer.

The enemy would be pleased for us to collect truth that never becomes surrender. Knowledge without obedience can make a person sound mature while the heart remains resistant. But the Word of God was never meant to make us impressive. It was meant to make us faithful.

There is grace for the person who has delayed obedience and now turns back. The Lord is not calling you to despair over what you have not done. He is calling you to obey today. The next step may be simple, but simple does not mean easy. It may cost pride, comfort, or control.

But obedience to Jesus is never wasted.

Ask honestly: what have I heard from God's Word that I have not yet obeyed?

Do not only admire the truth.

Do not only underline it.

Do not only talk about it.

Walk in it.

The blessing is not in hearing the Word while keeping control. It is in receiving the Word with a surrendered heart and allowing it to shape the next step.
