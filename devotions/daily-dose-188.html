Daily Dose #188

October 29, 2026

WALK AS HE WALKED

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on October 29, 2026.

1 JOHN 2:6 (ESV)

"Whoever says he abides in him ought to walk in the same way in which he walked."

John connects our confession to our conduct.

If we say we abide in Christ, our lives should increasingly reflect His way. We are called to walk as He walked. That is a serious calling, but it is also a clear one. Christianity is not only claiming His name. It is following His steps.

This does not mean we become sinless by our own effort. Scripture is clear that believers still need confession, forgiveness, correction, and growth. But true fellowship with Jesus changes the direction of a life.

His humility begins to challenge our pride.

His purity confronts our compromise.

His compassion softens our hardness.

His obedience reorders our priorities.

His truth exposes our excuses.

The world should not merely hear that we belong to Jesus. There should be evidence of His life shaping ours.

1 PETER 2:21 (ESV) says Christ left us "an example, so that you might follow in his steps."

Those steps include obedience when it cost Him, patience under suffering, truth without sin, compassion toward the needy, dependence on the Father, and holiness in a world full of darkness.

To walk as He walked means we cannot treat Jesus only as comfort while resisting Him as pattern. He is Saviour and Lord. He forgives, but He also leads. He receives sinners, but He does not leave them unchanged.

This searches the everyday life.

How did Jesus walk with the Father?

How did Jesus treat people?

How did Jesus respond to temptation?

How did Jesus handle rejection, pressure, misunderstanding, and suffering?

How did Jesus speak truth?

How did Jesus love?

The answer is not to imitate Him in our own strength while disconnected from Him. We walk as He walked because we abide in Him. The verse begins with abiding. The life comes from connection before it shows in conduct.

JOHN 15:5 (ESV) says, "I am the vine; you are the branches."

Branches bear fruit by remaining in the vine. The call to walk like Christ drives us deeper into dependence on Christ.

Ask where your walk does not match your words.

Not with condemnation, but with surrender.

COLOSSIANS 2:6 (ESV) says, "Therefore, as you received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk in him." The Christian walk begins and continues in Christ. We received Him by faith, and we walk in Him by faith. We do not move on from dependence after conversion. We go deeper into it.

This protects us from two errors. One error says conduct does not matter because grace covers everything. John will not allow that. If we abide in Him, our walk should change. The other error says change happens by our own strength, as though we can imitate Jesus while living disconnected from Him. John will not allow that either. The call is to abide and then walk.

1 JOHN 3:2-3 (ESV) says that when Christ appears, believers shall be like Him, and everyone who hopes in Him purifies himself as He is pure. Hope in Christ does not make holiness irrelevant. It makes holiness desirable. We are becoming like the One we are waiting to see.

So bring the ordinary walk before Him. The way you handle frustration. The way you use your words. The way you think when nobody hears. The way you treat people who cannot benefit you. The way you respond when obedience costs more than expected.

The confession becomes visible when the footsteps start changing.

This is a gracious challenge because it calls us away from empty words and toward living fellowship with Christ. If the walk is drifting, the answer is not to polish the confession while ignoring the direction. The answer is to return to Him. Abide again. Listen again. Surrender again.

Jesus does not merely point to a path and leave us alone. He is the way. He gives grace for obedience, forgiveness for failure, and strength for the next step. The more we behold Him in Scripture, the more our lives are corrected by His beauty.

Let the name you carry be seen in the way you walk.

The world has seen enough empty claims. What glorifies Christ is a life being slowly, truly conformed to Him. Not flawless, but honest. Not performative, but surrendered. Not merely saying His name, but walking in His way.

Abiding makes the walk possible, and the walk makes abiding visible.
