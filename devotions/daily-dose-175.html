Daily Dose #175

October 16, 2026

SERVE WITH WHAT YOU HAVE

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on October 16, 2026.

1 PETER 4:10 (ESV)

"As each has received a gift, use it to serve one another, as good stewards of God's varied grace."

God does not ask you to serve with what He gave someone else.

Peter says each has received a gift, and each is called to use it to serve others. What you have been given is not only for you. It is stewardship. It is grace entrusted to you so that others may be strengthened.

Comparison can make us bury what God entrusted to us.

We look at someone else's ability, confidence, platform, opportunity, knowledge, or personality and decide our own part is too small. We imagine that if we cannot serve in the same way they do, then what we have does not matter. But stewardship is not about having the most visible gift. It is about being faithful with what God placed in your hands.

Peter calls believers stewards of God's varied grace. That wording is humbling because a steward does not own the gift as though it exists for self-display. A steward receives, manages, and uses what belongs to another.

Grace comes in various forms. Some serve through teaching. Some through encouragement. Some through hospitality. Some through giving. Some through prayer. Some through practical help, administration, mercy, creativity, wisdom, or quiet faithfulness that keeps things strong behind the scenes.

The body of Christ needs more than one kind of gift.

1 CORINTHIANS 12:18 (ESV) says, "But as it is, God arranged the members in the body, each one of them, as he chose."

That means placement is not random. God knows what He is doing in the body. The hand does not need to become the eye. The foot does not need to become the ear. Each part has value because God designed the body with wisdom.

When comparison rules us, we may either become proud or discouraged. Proud when we think our gift is more important than someone else's. Discouraged when we think our gift is less important and therefore unnecessary. Both responses miss the point.

The gift is not about exalting self.

It is about serving others and honouring God.

Jesus said in MATTHEW 20:28 (ESV), "Even as the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve."

If the Son of Man came to serve, then service is not beneath the people who follow Him. We do not lose dignity by serving. We reflect Christ.

Ask what is in your hands. Not what you wish was in your hands. Not what someone else has. What has God entrusted to you? Who can be strengthened by it? Where can it be used without needing attention?

You may be waiting for a larger opportunity while neglecting a present one. You may be overlooking the ordinary ways God wants to use your life because they do not look impressive enough. But the Lord sees faithful stewardship.

Paul gives the same principle in ROMANS 12:6-8 (ESV) when he says that, having gifts that differ according to grace, believers should use them. The one who teaches should teach. The one who exhorts should exhort. The one who contributes should do so generously. The one who leads should lead with zeal. The one who does acts of mercy should do them with cheerfulness.

There is freedom in that. You do not have to force yourself into someone else's assignment to be useful in God's kingdom. You do not have to despise the grace given to you because it appears quieter. You do not have to wait until you feel impressive enough to serve.

But there is also responsibility. A gift neglected because of fear is still neglected. A gift hidden because of comparison is still hidden. A gift used for self-promotion is still being mishandled. Peter says we are stewards, which means the gift remains God's even while it is entrusted to us.

So serve without apology and without pride. Serve in dependence, because even the ability to serve is grace. Serve with prayer, because gifts need holiness. Serve with love, because people are not projects. Serve with endurance, because stewardship is often long and ordinary before it feels fruitful.

Use what you have.

Use it humbly.

Use it for others.

Use it for God.

The gift becomes worship when it stops needing to be compared.

Faithful service may also require courage. It can feel vulnerable to offer what God has given when you are not sure how it will be received. But fear is a poor steward. Bring the fear to the Lord, then take the next obedient step with humility.

You do not need to be impressive to be useful. You need to be faithful.
