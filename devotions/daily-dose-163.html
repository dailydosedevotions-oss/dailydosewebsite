Daily Dose #163

October 4, 2026

A GENTLE ANSWER

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on October 4, 2026.

PROVERBS 15:1 (ESV)

"A gentle answer turns away wrath, but a harsh word stirs up anger."

A gentle answer can feel costly when emotions are high.

When someone speaks sharply, everything in us may want to answer in the same spirit. Harshness invites harshness. Accusation invites defence. Sarcasm invites sarcasm. Before long, the conversation is no longer about truth or healing. It is about winning, protecting pride, and making sure the other person feels the force of our frustration.

Proverbs gives another way: "A gentle answer turns away wrath."

Gentleness is not weakness. It is not pretending wrong is right. It is not allowing sin to continue without truth. Biblical gentleness is strength under God's control. It is the ability to carry truth without adding unnecessary fire.

That is why a gentle answer can be so powerful. It interrupts the pattern. It refuses to let anger dictate the tone. It gives space for wisdom to enter a moment that could easily become destructive.

The second half of the verse is also true: "a harsh word stirs up anger."

Harsh words often feel satisfying for a few seconds and damaging for much longer. They may win an argument while wounding a heart. They may express frustration while making reconciliation harder. They may feel justified in the moment, but they often leave behind more smoke than light.

JAMES 3:17 (ESV) says that wisdom from above is "peaceable, gentle, open to reason."

Gentleness belongs to heavenly wisdom. That means it is not merely a personality trait for naturally quiet people. It is something God forms in those who belong to Him. A strong-willed person can become gentle by the Spirit. A quick-tempered person can learn self-control. A wounded person can learn to speak without using pain as permission to wound others.

This does not mean every conversation will end peacefully. You can answer gently and the other person may still choose anger. You can speak wisely and someone may still reject it. Peace is not always fully in your control.

But your answer still matters before God.

ROMANS 12:18 (ESV) says, "If possible, so far as it depends on you, live peaceably with all." That verse is honest. It says, "if possible," because sometimes peace requires more than one willing heart. But it also says, "so far as it depends on you." Your part still matters.

There are moments when obedience is not found in having the final word. It is found in having a surrendered word.

This is difficult because harshness can feel like protection. If someone speaks sharply to us, gentleness may feel like losing. But the cross teaches us that obedience is not measured by whether pride feels protected. Jesus entrusted Himself to the Father even while being mistreated. He did not answer sin with sin.

1 PETER 2:23 (ESV) says, "When he was reviled, he did not revile in return."

That does not mean every believer must remain silent in the face of wrongdoing. Jesus spoke truth boldly. But He did not let the sin of others determine the holiness of His response. That is the deeper issue. Will another person's harshness govern your mouth, or will the Spirit of God?

There is also humility in admitting when we have stirred up anger instead of turning it away. Sometimes the most faithful next step is not another explanation, but repentance. A gentle answer may need to begin with, "I was wrong in how I spoke." That kind of humility can feel costly, but pride is far more costly in the end.

MATTHEW 5:9 (ESV) says, "Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called sons of God."

Peacemaking is not pretending there is no conflict. It is bringing a different spirit into conflict because we belong to God.

Before you respond, pause long enough to ask: will this answer turn away wrath or stir it up? Am I speaking from wisdom or from the need to hurt back? Can truth be spoken here without harshness leading it?

The Lord does not call us to powerless speech. He calls us to Spirit-formed speech.

Gentleness may be one of the clearest signs that we have stopped needing anger to feel strong. A surrendered answer does not have to be weak, vague, or afraid. It can be truthful and still be governed by love. It can be firm and still be free from cruelty.

Ask the Lord to train your tongue before the tense moment arrives. The answer formed in prayer will be different from the answer formed in pride.

Do not add fire just because fire was handed to you.
