Quiet Trust in the Waiting: Part 2

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on November 5, 2026.

PSALM 130:5-6 (ESV)

"I wait for the LORD, my soul waits, and in his word I hope."

Waiting becomes dangerous when hope is cut loose from the Word of God.

If hope is only built on what we want to happen, it can become fragile. If it is built on a certain timeline, it can collapse when the answer takes longer. If it is built on a feeling, it can rise and fall with tiredness, disappointment, or fear.

But Scripture gives us a better anchor.

In PSALM 130:5 (ESV), the psalmist says, "I wait for the LORD, my soul waits, and in his word I hope." This is not empty waiting. It is not passive despair. It is waiting that has somewhere to stand.

The hope is in God's Word.

That matters because God's Word tells the truth when our thoughts are unstable. The mind may say, "Nothing is changing." The Word says the Lord is faithful. The mind may say, "I am forgotten." The Word says the Lord hears the cry of His people. The mind may say, "This delay means there is no mercy." The Word reveals a God who is steadfast in love.

Psalm 130 begins from the depths. This is not a shallow song from someone untouched by trouble. The psalmist cries to the Lord from a low place and asks Him to hear. Waiting in Scripture is not always peaceful in the emotional sense. Sometimes it is tearful, weak, and desperate. But it is still faith when the cry is directed to God.

PSALM 130:6 (ESV) says, "my soul waits for the Lord more than watchmen for the morning." Watchmen did not create the morning. They could not hurry the sun. But they watched with certainty that night would not have the final word.

That is a strong picture of biblical waiting.

The believer does not control the dawn, but the believer knows the Lord rules over it. Waiting on God does not mean the night feels short. It means the heart refuses to believe the night is sovereign.

This is where the Word of God steadies us.

ROMANS 15:4 (ESV) says that through the encouragement of the Scriptures "we might have hope." Scripture does not merely inform the mind. The Holy Spirit uses the Word to strengthen hope, correct false conclusions, and lift the eyes back to God.

There will be times when your thoughts preach to you loudly. They may preach fear, accusation, pressure, or despair. But the Word must be allowed to speak louder.

Waiting well does not mean you never feel the weight of the delay. It means you keep bringing the weight under the authority of what God has spoken.

Let the Word shape what waiting means.

Not abandonment. Not uselessness. Not the absence of God.

Waiting can be a place where hope is purified, where the soul learns to look for the Lord more than for quick relief, and where faith becomes anchored in something stronger than visible change.

The morning belongs to God.

So wait for the Lord, and let your hope be in His Word.
