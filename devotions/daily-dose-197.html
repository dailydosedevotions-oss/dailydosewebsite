Daily Dose #197

November 7, 2026

What Shall I Render?

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on November 7, 2026.

PSALM 116:12-14 (ESV)

"What shall I render to the LORD for all his benefits to me?"

Gratitude becomes deeper when it turns into surrendered living.

In PSALM 116:12 (ESV), the psalmist asks, "What shall I render to the LORD for all his benefits to me?" It is not the question of someone trying to pay God back, as though mercy can be repaid. It is the question of a heart awakened to grace.

The Lord had dealt bountifully with him. The psalm speaks of distress, tears, stumbling, and deliverance. This gratitude was not shallow. It came from someone who knew what it meant to be helped by God.

That is often how true worship grows. When we remember what the Lord has done, the heart begins to ask how life should respond. Not just words. Not just songs. Not just a moment of emotion. A whole life brought before God.

The psalmist answers with worship, calling on the name of the Lord and paying vows in the presence of God's people. Gratitude becomes public faithfulness. The mercy received in private begins to shape obedience in the open.

That challenges us.

It is possible to enjoy God's gifts while withholding our lives from Him. We can thank Him for help, then return to self-rule. We can rejoice in answered prayer, then resist the surrender He is calling for. We can speak of His goodness, yet keep parts of our heart closed.

But mercy is meant to move us.

ROMANS 12:1 (ESV) says to present our bodies as a living sacrifice "by the mercies of God." Notice the order. Surrender is not driven by earning. It is driven by mercy. We give ourselves to God because He has first been merciful to us.

The question is not, "How can I repay the Lord?" You cannot. Christ alone has done what sinners could never do for themselves. The question is, "How should mercy shape me now?"

It should shape your words. Your priorities. Your repentance. Your generosity. Your obedience when no one is applauding. Your willingness to forgive. Your worship when life is ordinary. Your faithfulness when feelings are not loud.

Gratitude that remains only a feeling can fade quickly. Gratitude that becomes surrender bears fruit.

2 CORINTHIANS 5:15 (ESV) says Christ died for all, "that those who live might no longer live for themselves but for him." That is the shape of grace. The saved life becomes a yielded life.

So ask the question honestly before the Lord.

What has He delivered you from? What has He carried you through? What mercy are you too quick to forget? And how should your life answer?

Not with payment.

With worshipful surrender.
