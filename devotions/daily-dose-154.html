Daily Dose #154

September 25, 2026

STILL LEARNING TO TRUST

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on September 25, 2026.

PSALM 37:3-5 (ESV)

"Trust in the LORD, and do good; dwell in the land and befriend faithfulness. Delight yourself in the LORD, and he will give you the desires of your heart. Commit your way to the LORD; trust in him, and he will act."

Trust is not only something we say.

Psalm 37 connects trust with doing good, dwelling faithfully, delighting in the Lord, and committing our way to Him. Trust has movement. It shows up in how we live while we wait.

There are seasons where trusting God feels simple, and there are seasons where it feels like something we must learn again every morning. That does not mean faith has failed. It means the heart is being invited deeper. Trust is not only proven when everything feels calm. Trust is often formed when the answer is not yet visible and obedience still matters.

David says, "Trust in the LORD, and do good."

That pairing matters. Sometimes when we are waiting, confused, or disappointed, we stop doing the good that is in front of us. We become so focused on what God has not yet revealed that we neglect what He has already made clear.

But trust does not freeze obedience.

Trust keeps doing good while the outcome is still in God's hands. Trust keeps telling the truth. Trust keeps forgiving. Trust keeps praying. Trust keeps serving. Trust keeps walking in the light God has already given, even before the next part of the road is visible.

The psalm also says, "dwell in the land and befriend faithfulness."

There is something steady about that. Trust does not always run to the next thing. Sometimes trust remains faithful where God has placed you. It stops trying to escape every uncomfortable season and asks, "Lord, what does obedience look like here?"

That is not always what we want. Sometimes we want relief more than formation. We want the next place, the next answer, the next open door. But God may be teaching us to dwell faithfully before He moves us visibly. He may be forming trust in the very place we are tempted to rush through.

Then David says, "Commit your way to the LORD."

Control demands the whole road before obeying. Commitment places the road into God's hands and takes the next faithful step. Control says, "I need to know how this will work out before I can trust." Commitment says, "I may not know how this will work out, but I know the One I am entrusting it to."

That does not mean we invent promises God has not spoken. We have to be careful there. Trusting God is not the same as assuming He will do everything according to our preferred plan. Scripture does not call us to build confidence on imagination. It calls us to build confidence on God's character and His revealed Word.

PROVERBS 3:5-6 (ESV) says, "Trust in the LORD with all your heart, and do not lean on your own understanding."

That is not an invitation to switch off wisdom. It is a command not to make our own understanding lord. There will be times when what God is doing is beyond what we can explain. There will be times when obedience is clear but the outcome is not. There will be times when our understanding is limited, but God's faithfulness is not.

Trust learns to obey without needing to control the whole story.

This can be especially hard when waiting has been long. A delayed answer can tempt the heart to take matters into its own hands. We may start forcing doors, manipulating outcomes, or calling anxiety wisdom because we are afraid to leave the matter with God.

But Scripture gives us another way.

1 PETER 5:7 (ESV) says, "casting all your anxieties on him, because he cares for you."

God does not tell His people to cast anxieties on Him because He is distant. He says to cast them on Him because He cares. That means surrender is not throwing our concerns into emptiness. It is placing them into the hands of a Father who is faithful, wise, and near.

Maybe there is an area of your life where trust still feels unfinished. You believe God, but your hands keep tightening. You pray, but then you immediately try to force the answer. You say you have surrendered, but your mind keeps taking the weight back.

Bring that honestly to Him.

Do not pretend trust is stronger than it is. The Lord is not helped by pretending. He meets us truthfully. You can say, "Lord, I believe; help my unbelief," and that prayer is not foreign to Scripture.

Trust may tremble, but it keeps turning toward God.

The Lord is patient with those who are still learning to trust. He does not despise the faith that keeps coming back to Him. But He does call us out of anxious striving and into deeper surrender.

Commit your way to Him again.

Not because you understand everything.

Because He is faithful with what you cannot hold.
