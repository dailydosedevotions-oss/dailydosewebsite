Daily Dose #171

October 12, 2026

HONOUR ONE ANOTHER

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on October 12, 2026.

ROMANS 12:10 (ESV)

"Love one another with brotherly affection. Outdo one another in showing honor."

Honour is love with humility in it.

Romans calls believers to be devoted to one another and to prefer one another in honour. That cuts against the selfish instinct to compete, compare, or always place ourselves first.

Honouring others does not mean pretending everyone is right. It does not mean ignoring sin, flattering people, or avoiding truth. It means refusing to treat people as obstacles, tools, or background characters in our own story. Every person is made in the image of God. Every brother or sister in Christ is someone for whom Jesus died.

That should change the way we speak about people.

It should change the way we listen.

It should change the way we respond when someone else is celebrated.

Comparison makes honour difficult. When we are insecure, another person's growth can feel threatening. Their open door can feel like our loss. Their gift can make us question our own. Their encouragement can expose how much we wanted to be noticed.

But love does not need to compete.

PHILIPPIANS 2:3 (ESV) says, "Do nothing from selfish ambition or conceit, but in humility count others more significant than yourselves."

That is not natural to the flesh. The flesh wants to be first, seen, praised, and protected. The Spirit forms a different way. He teaches us to honour without needing to be diminished by someone else's blessing.

In ordinary life, honour can look like listening well instead of waiting to speak. It can look like speaking respectfully even in disagreement. It can look like celebrating someone's growth without adding a hidden criticism. It can look like refusing gossip. It can look like making room for another person's voice. It can look like serving without needing recognition.

Honour also shows up in the unseen tone of the heart.

You can say polite words while despising someone inwardly. You can outwardly congratulate while inwardly resenting. You can appear kind while secretly hoping someone fails. But God sees deeper than behaviour. He forms honour from the inside out.

1 PETER 2:17 (ESV) says, "Honor everyone. Love the brotherhood. Fear God. Honor the emperor."

Honour is not reserved only for people who are easy to honour. The fear of God teaches us to treat people with seriousness, dignity, and humility because we live before Him.

This does not remove discernment. Some relationships require boundaries. Honour does not mean trusting everyone equally or giving unsafe people access they should not have. But even boundaries can be carried without hatred, contempt, or revenge.

The flesh asks, "How can I be seen?"

Love asks, "How can I honour God in the way I treat this person?"

Where comparison has made your heart tight, let honour make it free.

James gives a needed warning here. JAMES 2:1 (ESV) says, "My brothers, show no partiality as you hold the faith in our Lord Jesus Christ, the Lord of glory." Honour is not meant to be given only to the impressive, useful, wealthy, gifted, popular, or powerful. The glory of Christ rebukes the way we rank people according to worldly value.

This searches church life deeply. Do we honour the person who serves quietly as much as the person who is visible? Do we notice the lonely, the awkward, the grieving, the elderly, the young, the new believer, the one who cannot give us anything back? Do we treat people differently when they have influence? Partiality can hide under politeness, but God sees the heart.

Honour also changes disagreement. You can hold a firm conviction without stripping someone of dignity. You can speak truth without contempt. You can correct without mockery. You can set a boundary without hatred. The fear of God teaches us that people are not disposable.

Christ honoured the lowly. He welcomed those others overlooked. He corrected the proud and drew near to the broken. If His life is shaping ours, honour will begin to show in where we look, whom we notice, how we speak, and whether love has room for people who do not benefit us.

Honour can begin quietly. Use the person's name with care. Listen without rushing. Refuse the joke that lowers someone for a laugh. Speak well of a brother or sister when they are not in the room. Make space for another person's gift without needing to prove your own.

The kingdom of God does not train us to climb over people. It teaches us to love them before the Lord.
