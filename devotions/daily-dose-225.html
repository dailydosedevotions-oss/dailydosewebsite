God With Us

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on December 5, 2026.

MATTHEW 1:22-23 (ESV)

"They shall call his name Immanuel."

The promise of Christmas is not that God sent advice from far away. In MATTHEW 1:23 (ESV), Matthew says the child will be called Immanuel, which means "God with us." Those words are not soft decoration. They are deep doctrine and deep comfort. The eternal Son truly came. God drew near in the person of Jesus Christ. This nearness must not be made vague. God with us does not mean God approving everything we do. It does not mean God is merely a warm feeling in the room. It means the promised Savior has come, fully God and truly man, to redeem His people. Matthew quotes the promise from Isaiah and shows its fulfilment in Christ. The birth of Jesus is not random. It is God's faithful action in history. That matters when life feels unstable. Our hope is not built on a seasonal mood. It is built on the fact that God has acted. Christ has come. The Son has taken on flesh. The Savior has entered the world He came to redeem.

Immanuel also confronts loneliness at the deepest level. People can be surrounded and still feel unseen. They can be busy and still feel hollow. They can carry burdens no one fully understands. But the gospel announces a nearness stronger than human attention. HEBREWS 4:15 (ESV) says we do not have a high priest who is unable to sympathize with our weaknesses. Jesus is not distant from human frailty. He entered real human life without sin, and He is able to help His people. Still, the greatest need met by Immanuel is not emotional comfort only. We needed salvation. We needed God to come near because sin had separated us from Him. Christ came not merely to stand beside us in difficulty, but to bring us back to God. If December feels noisy, let this truth become steady in you: God has come near in Christ. Not because humanity climbed high enough. Because mercy came down.
