Words Seasoned With Grace

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on April 29, 2027.

COLOSSIANS 4:5–6 (ESV)

“Let your speech always be gracious, seasoned with salt.”

Paul connects gracious speech with the way Christians relate to people outside the faith. The message matters, and so does the manner.

Grace does not mean avoiding difficult truth. Jesus spoke clearly about sin, judgment, forgiveness and the cost of following Him. Yet He did not use truth as a weapon to make Himself feel superior.

Speech seasoned with salt has wisdom and purpose. It is not bland, careless or corrupting. It knows when to answer, when to ask a question and when to remain quiet.

Before speaking about faith, listen. Learn what the other person actually believes instead of answering assumptions. Avoid Christian phrases they may not understand, or explain them when they matter. Do not treat honest questions as attacks.

The goal is not to make the gospel easier by changing it. The goal is to remove unnecessary offence caused by our pride, impatience or poor listening.

Speak truth clearly. Carry it graciously.
