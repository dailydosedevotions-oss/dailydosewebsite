Daily Dose #162

October 3, 2026

SEASONED WITH SALT

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on October 3, 2026.

COLOSSIANS 4:6 (ESV)

"Let your speech always be gracious, seasoned with salt, so that you may know how you ought to answer each person."

Not every answer should sound the same.

Paul tells believers that our speech should be "with grace, seasoned with salt." That means Christian speech is not careless, bland, cowardly, or harsh. It carries grace, but it also carries wisdom. It has truth in it. It has discernment in it. It considers the person, the moment, and what faithfulness requires.

Some conversations need gentleness. Some need clarity. Some need courage. Some need patience. Some need fewer words than we were planning to use. Some need us to listen longer before we answer at all.

The goal is not to sound impressive. The goal is to honour Christ.

Paul says this kind of speech helps us know "how you ought to answer each one." That phrase reminds us that people are not projects. Each one matters. Each conversation needs wisdom. We cannot simply throw words around because we know something true.

Truth must still be spoken truthfully.

Grace must still be full of grace.

Jesus is the perfect picture of this. JOHN 1:14 (ESV) says that the Word became flesh and was "full of grace and truth." In Christ, grace and truth are not enemies. He never compromised truth, and He never lacked holy love.

We need that deeply in our speech.

There are people who use truth like a weapon because they are not submitted to love. There are others who avoid truth because they are afraid of discomfort. Neither reflects the fullness of Christ. The Lord forms people who can speak what is true with a heart that has been humbled by grace.

PROVERBS 15:23 (ESV) says, "To make an apt answer is a joy to a man, and a word in season, how good it is!"

Timing matters. Tone matters. Motive matters. A right word spoken at the wrong time may not land rightly. A true word spoken from pride can still do harm. A gentle word spoken with wisdom can open a door that force would have slammed shut.

This applies everywhere. In the home. In messages. In disagreements. In ministry. In friendships. Online. In moments where you feel misunderstood. In moments where someone asks a question and you are tempted to answer quickly without love.

Before you speak, ask God for wisdom.

What is needed here?

Is this the right moment?

Am I answering this person, or am I reacting to my own frustration?

Am I trying to win, or am I trying to be faithful?

There is a difference between being bold and being careless. There is a difference between being gentle and being afraid. The Spirit of God teaches us the difference as we submit our mouths to Him.

Salt preserves. Salt gives flavour. Salt can also sting where there is a wound. That is why it must be governed by grace. Speech seasoned with salt does not avoid truth, but it refuses to become corrupt, proud, or reckless.

Today, do not only ask whether you have something to say.

Ask whether your answer has been seasoned by time with the Lord. A heart that has not listened to God will struggle to answer people well. We need more than quick opinions. We need speech that has been humbled by Scripture, softened by grace, and strengthened by truth.

This matters especially when correction is needed. Correction without grace can become crushing. Grace without truth can become shallow. But when the Spirit forms both in us, our words can become faithful without becoming cruel.

EPHESIANS 4:15 (ESV) speaks of "speaking the truth in love."

That is not a slogan. It is a calling. Truth and love must travel together in the mouth of a believer. Truth without love can become a hammer. Love without truth can become a hiding place. But speech shaped by Christ carries both.

This also means we cannot use personality as an excuse. Some people say, "I just say it how it is," but Scripture does not give us permission to speak truth without self-control. Others say, "I do not like confrontation," but Scripture does not give us permission to avoid truth when love requires it. Both the bold and the quiet need the Lord to season their speech.

The question is not whether your natural style feels justified. The question is whether your words are submitted to Christ.

Ask whether the Lord is shaping how you say it.
