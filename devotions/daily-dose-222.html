The Silence Breaks

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on December 2, 2026.

LUKE 1:13-17 (ESV)

"Your prayer has been heard."

There are prayers that seem to disappear into silence. Zechariah and Elizabeth knew years of waiting. They were righteous before God, yet they carried a grief that had not lifted. Luke tells us Elizabeth was barren, and both were advanced in years. Humanly speaking, the door looked closed. Then the angel says in LUKE 1:13 (ESV), "your prayer has been heard." Those words are tender. They do not mean Zechariah and Elizabeth understood the timing. They do not mean the waiting was easy. They mean heaven had not been deaf. God had heard, and His answer would fit into something larger than their private longing. Their son John would not merely bring joy to his parents. He would go before the Lord, turning hearts and preparing a people. What felt personal was being gathered into the purposes of God. This does not mean every delayed prayer will be answered exactly as we hope. Scripture never gives us permission to force every story into the same outcome. But this passage does teach us that delay is not proof God has not heard. The Lord is able to work in hidden years. He is able to weave private pain into public mercy. He is able to answer at a time that reveals His wisdom rather than ours. He is able to bring forth purpose from a place that seemed past possibility.

PSALM 34:15 (ESV) says, "The eyes of the LORD are toward the righteous and his ears toward their cry." That is not a promise that every answer will arrive quickly. It is a promise that the Lord is not indifferent to the cry of His people. Zechariah struggled to believe the announcement. That is honest too. Sometimes the answer comes after so much waiting that faith itself feels startled. But God's faithfulness is not weakened by human surprise. If you are in a long silence, do not build your theology on the silence alone. Build it on the God who hears. The silence may feel long. But the Lord has not stopped ruling, hearing, or preparing.
