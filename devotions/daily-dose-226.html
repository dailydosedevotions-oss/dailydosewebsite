A Savior for Sinners

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on December 6, 2026.

MATTHEW 1:21 (ESV)

"You shall call his name Jesus, for he will save his people from their sins."

Jesus was not born merely to inspire better behaviour. The angel tells Joseph in MATTHEW 1:21 (ESV), "you shall call his name Jesus, for he will save his people from their sins." That statement gives the mission of Christ with clarity. The deepest human problem is not inconvenience, lack of motivation, low self-esteem, or difficult circumstances. The deepest problem is sin. That is not comfortable to say, but it is merciful. If we misdiagnose the problem, we will misunderstand the Savior. Jesus did not come only to improve our mood, support our dreams, or decorate our traditions. He came to save. From sin.

Sin is not only the obvious things people hide. It is rebellion against God. It is unbelief, pride, idolatry, selfishness, impurity, bitterness, lovelessness, and the countless ways the heart turns from the Lord. Sin leaves guilt before a holy God and bondage within the human heart. Christ came for sinners who could not save themselves. That makes Christmas deeply humbling. The manger is beautiful, but it points toward the cross. The child born in Bethlehem came to bear sin, satisfy justice, defeat death, and bring His people to God.

1 PETER 3:18 (ESV) says, "Christ also suffered once for sins, the righteous for the unrighteous, that he might bring us to God." That is salvation. Not God pretending sin does not matter. Not sinners saving themselves through effort. The righteous One suffered for the unrighteous. This truth also gives hope to those who feel too stained to come. Jesus is not surprised that sinners need saving. That is why He came. Do not hide behind religious appearance or despair. Come to Him in repentance and faith. The name Jesus carries rescue in it. He saves His people from their sins. Not partly. Not cosmetically. Truly. That also means no sinner should treat conviction as hopeless. If Christ came to save from sin, then exposed sin is not an invitation to hide deeper; it is an invitation to come into the mercy He came to bring. LUKE 5:32 (ESV) records Jesus saying, "I have not come to call the righteous but sinners to repentance." The Savior is not looking for people who can pretend they never needed saving. He calls sinners to come honestly, turn from sin, and find life in Him.
