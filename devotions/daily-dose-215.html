Daily Dose #215

November 25, 2026

Godliness With Contentment

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on November 25, 2026.

1 TIMOTHY 6:6-8 (ESV)

"But godliness with contentment is great gain."

Contentment is not laziness, and it is not lack of desire for good things.

Biblical contentment is the heart learning to be settled in God rather than ruled by craving. In 1 TIMOTHY 6:6 (ESV), Paul says, "godliness with contentment is great gain." That sentence confronts a world that often defines gain by having more, being seen more, owning more, and never feeling satisfied.

Paul is not against provision. He is warning against the love of money and the restless desire that pulls the heart away from God. Contentment protects worship.

The next verses remind us that we brought nothing into the world and cannot take anything out of it. That truth is simple but sobering. So much of what people chase cannot be carried beyond death. Earthly gain has limits. Godliness has eternal weight.

Contentment does not mean refusing to work, plan, grow, or steward what God gives. Scripture commends diligence. But diligence and discontentment are not the same thing. You can work faithfully without worshiping increase. You can ask God for provision without making provision your peace.

Discontentment often speaks with the language of "not enough."

Not enough money. Not enough recognition. Not enough comfort. Not enough progress. Not enough of what someone else has. The heart can become trained to notice lack more than mercy.

Contentment does not deny need. It brings need before God without letting need become master.

PHILIPPIANS 4:12-13 (ESV) says Paul learned the secret of facing plenty and hunger, abundance and need, through Christ who strengthened him. Contentment was learned. That should encourage us. If contentment does not come naturally, you are not unusual. The Lord teaches it.

He teaches it as we remember what is eternal. He teaches it as we receive daily bread with gratitude. He teaches it as we repent of comparison. He teaches it as we see Christ as better than every gift.

If your heart has been restless, do not only ask, "What do I lack?" Ask, "What has my heart started believing I must have in order to be okay?"

That question can reveal hidden idols.

Christ is not against your good. He is your good. The gifts of God are blessings, but they cannot become the foundation of your soul.

Godliness with contentment is great gain because it frees the heart to live before God instead of being dragged by endless wanting.

The satisfied soul is not the one that owns everything.

It is the one held by Christ.
