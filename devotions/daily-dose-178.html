A HOUSE THAT SERVES THE LORD

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on October 19, 2026.

JOSHUA 24:15 (ESV)

"Choose this day whom you will serve... But as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD."

Joshua's words are not a wall decoration first.

They are a declaration of direction: "But as for me and my house, we will serve the LORD."

Those words were spoken in a moment of decision. God's people had to choose whom they would serve. Joshua made it clear that his household would belong to the Lord. Not perfectly, not casually, not only in language, but in direction.

Serving the Lord in a home is not only about public moments or spiritual phrases. It is seen in the daily life of the household. In forgiveness. In patience. In worship. In truth. In repentance. In prayer. In priorities. In what is allowed, what is resisted, what is celebrated, and how people are treated behind closed doors.

A house that serves the Lord is not a perfect house.

It is a surrendered one.

That distinction matters. No family, marriage, home, or household is without weakness. There will be mistakes, tired days, hard conversations, and moments that require repentance. The question is not whether everything looks flawless. The question is whether the Lord is truly honoured there.

Is His Word given authority?

Is sin excused or brought into the light?

Is forgiveness practiced?

Is prayer normal or only used in crisis?

Is the atmosphere shaped more by the fear of the Lord or by pressure, pride, anger, and distraction?

PSALM 127:1 (ESV) says, "Unless the LORD builds the house, those who build it labor in vain."

A home needs more than effort. It needs the Lord. We can work hard to build routines, provide needs, create comfort, and manage responsibilities, but if the Lord is not honoured, the foundation is wrong.

This begins personally.

Joshua did not wait for everyone else before choosing faithfulness. He said, "as for me." There are decisions you can make before God even if you cannot control every person around you. You can choose to serve the Lord in your speech. You can choose repentance. You can choose prayer. You can choose obedience. You can choose to make the Word of God central.

PSALM 78:6-7 (ESV) speaks of the coming generation setting "their hope in God."

God's Word was meant to shape the ordinary rhythms of life. Not only formal moments. Not only church settings. The home is one of the places where faith becomes visible in everyday ways.

This is a needed challenge.

It is easy to want a Christian-looking home while resisting the daily surrender that makes Christ honoured there. It is easy to put up words about serving the Lord while allowing bitterness, harshness, compromise, or prayerlessness to rule the atmosphere.

Let the declaration become more than a sentence.

Let it become a pattern.

As for me and my house, not merely in appearance, not merely in intention, but in truth, we will serve the Lord.

PROVERBS 24:3-4 (ESV) says, "By wisdom a house is built, and by understanding it is established." A household that serves the Lord needs wisdom from the Lord. Love alone, if it is not guided by truth, can become permissive. Rules alone, if they are not shaped by grace, can become hard. A Christ-honouring home needs both truth and tenderness.

This applies whether your household is large, small, new, complicated, peaceful, or in need of repair. The Lord is able to build what human strength cannot build by itself. He can teach a family how to repent. He can restore warmth where harshness has settled. He can make Scripture central again. He can show where old patterns need to be broken and where new rhythms need to be formed.

Serving the Lord will not happen accidentally. Something will shape the house. Screens will shape it. Anger will shape it. Fear will shape it. Busyness will shape it. Prayer will shape it. The Word will shape it. Worship will shape it. The question is not whether the home is being formed, but what is forming it.

So choose deliberately. Not with pressure to create an image, but with reverence before God. Let Christ be honoured in the unseen places. Let children, family, guests, and anyone who enters encounter something of His peace, truth, patience, and love.

COLOSSIANS 3:17 (ESV) says, "And whatever you do, in word or deed, do everything in the name of the Lord Jesus." That reaches the home. It reaches the ordinary words spoken when everyone is tired. It reaches how correction is given, how forgiveness is offered, how meals are shared, how disagreements are handled, and how priorities are chosen.

A house that serves the Lord is formed by repeated surrender. Not one dramatic declaration only, but daily decisions that say Christ is worthy here too. Worthy in the schedule. Worthy in the tone. Worthy in the private habits. Worthy in what is celebrated and what is refused.

May the sentence on the wall become the truth in the rooms.
