Daily Dose #173

October 14, 2026

BEARING WITH ONE ANOTHER

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on October 14, 2026.

COLOSSIANS 3:12-13 (ESV)

"Put on then, as God's chosen ones, holy and beloved, compassionate hearts, kindness, humility, meekness, and patience."

Relationships require more than warm feelings.

Paul tells God's people to put on compassion, kindness, humility, meekness, patience, and forgiveness. These are not decorations for an already easy life. They are necessary clothing for life together.

The command begins with identity: "God's chosen ones, holy and beloved." That matters. Paul does not tell believers to put on these things in order to become loved by God. He tells them to put them on because they already belong to Him. Grace becomes the ground for the new way of living.

We are chosen, holy, and beloved. Therefore, we do not keep wearing the old ways as though they still fit.

Compassion fits the people of God. Kindness fits. Humility fits. Patience fits. Forgiveness fits. Bitterness does not. Pride does not. Cruelty does not. Harshness does not. A refusal to forgive does not.

Paul says we are to bear with one another.

That phrase is practical because people are not always easy. Bearing with one another means love does not quit at the first inconvenience. It does not mean enabling sin or ignoring harm. It does not mean pretending serious wrongs are small. But it does mean we learn patience because God has been patient with us.

In real relationships, there will be weaknesses, misunderstandings, immaturity, differences, and disappointments. If love only remains when people never inconvenience us, then it is not the love Scripture is describing.

EPHESIANS 4:2 (ESV) says we are to walk "with all humility and gentleness, with patience, bearing with one another in love."

Bearing with one another must be done in love. Not with silent resentment. Not with a superior attitude. Not with a secret record of everything we are tolerating. Love carries patience without turning it into pride.

Then Paul speaks directly about forgiveness, telling believers to forgive each other when one has a complaint against another.

That is the standard: as Christ forgave you.

Forgiveness is not pretending sin did not matter. The cross proves sin matters deeply. Forgiveness is not saying consequences never exist. It is not refusing wisdom, boundaries, or truth. But forgiveness does mean we release the right to revenge. We stop feeding bitterness as though it will heal us. We remember the mercy we have received.

EPHESIANS 4:32 (ESV) says, "Be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, as God in Christ forgave you."

The forgiveness of Christ humbles us. We are not people who have only been wronged. We are people who have also needed mercy. That does not erase the pain others have caused, but it does keep bitterness from becoming lord.

Some days, these things must be put on deliberately. Compassion may not feel natural. Patience may not rise automatically. Forgiveness may feel costly. That is why Paul uses the language of clothing. We put on what fits the new life, even when the old reactions feel closer.

And above all of it, Paul says to put on love. COLOSSIANS 3:14 (ESV) says, "And above all these put on love, which binds everything together in perfect harmony." Love does not remove the need for truth, patience, humility, or forgiveness. It binds them together so they are not carried in a harsh or self-righteous way.

This is especially important when the same weakness keeps appearing in someone else. Bearing with one another does not mean pretending the weakness is good. It means we remember that sanctification is often slow, and we do not appoint ourselves as the impatient judge of someone else's process. We can speak truth, hold boundaries, and still refuse contempt.

There are also times when the person you need to bear with is not far away. It may be someone in your home, church, family, workplace, or close circle. The place where patience is most tested is often the place where love most needs to be practiced. It is easier to admire patience from a distance than to wear it in the conversation that has already tired you.

Ask the Lord to show you what you have been wearing. Bitterness has a tone. Pride has a tone. Impatience has a tone. So does compassion. So does kindness. So does humility. The people closest to us often experience what we are truly clothed in.

Before you respond to people today, remember what you have received.

Chosen.

Holy.

Beloved.

Forgiven.

Now wear what belongs to the life Christ has given you.
