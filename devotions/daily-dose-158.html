WHAT YOU BUILD ON

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on September 29, 2026.

MATTHEW 7:24-25 (ESV)

"Everyone then who hears these words of mine and does them will be like a wise man who built his house on the rock."

Jesus speaks about two houses.

From the outside, they may have looked similar for a while. Both were houses. Both had structure. Both may have appeared stable when the weather was calm. But the difference was not fully revealed until the rain came, the floods rose, and the winds beat against them.

One stood because it was built on rock.

The other fell because its foundation could not hold.

Jesus is not only talking about storms. He is talking about foundations. He says the wise person hears His words and does them. The foolish person hears His words and does not do them. The difference is obedience.

That means the foundation is not merely hearing the words of Jesus. It is hearing and doing them.

This is searching because it is possible to be familiar with truth without being submitted to it. It is possible to know what Jesus said, admire what Jesus said, quote what Jesus said, and still refuse to obey what Jesus said. But hearing without obedience does not build the same foundation.

Storms have a way of exposing what a life has really been built on.

If we build on feelings, we will be unstable when feelings change. If we build on approval, we will be shaken when people misunderstand us. If we build on comfort, we will panic when life becomes costly. If we build on outward religion without surrender, the structure may look good until pressure reveals the ground beneath it.

Jesus is not calling us to fear the storm. He is calling us to build wisely before it comes.

1 CORINTHIANS 3:11 (ESV) says, "For no one can lay a foundation other than that which is laid, which is Jesus Christ."

Christ is not one support among many. He is the foundation. Everything else must be brought under Him. Our decisions, relationships, habits, motives, priorities, private lives, ambitions, and responses all have to come under His lordship.

Many people want the peace of the rock while still building with sand. They want stability without surrender. They want the comfort of Jesus' promises without the authority of Jesus' words. They want Him as refuge, but not as Lord over the hidden rooms of the heart.

But Jesus does not separate hearing from obedience.

To build on the rock means we allow His Word to confront us. When He tells us to forgive, we do not keep rehearsing revenge. When He tells us to seek first the kingdom, we do not keep worshipping lesser things. When He tells us to love our enemies, we do not baptise bitterness. When He calls us to purity, truth, humility, generosity, and obedience, we do not treat His commands as optional advice.

The wise builder obeys.

This does not mean the wise builder never struggles. Obedience can be costly. There are times when doing what Jesus says feels harder than taking the easier road. But the easier road can still be sand. It may feel smoother at first, but it cannot hold the weight of a life.

There is a mercy in building before the storm comes. Nobody wants to think about foundations when the sky is clear, but that is exactly when foundations are being laid. Daily choices matter. Private obedience matters. What you do with conviction matters. Whether Scripture has authority over your desires matters.

The storm does not usually give time to rebuild everything from the ground up.

That is why Jesus gives this warning with love.

JOHN 13:17 (ESV) says, "If you know these things, blessed are you if you do them."

Knowing without doing creates a dangerous illusion. A person may feel safe because they know the truth, but Jesus says blessing is connected to doing what He has taught. The issue is not whether the words of Jesus are beautiful. They are. The issue is whether they are foundation.

This is not a call to legalism. We are not saved by building well enough for God to accept us. Christ is the foundation, and salvation is by grace. But the grace that saves us also brings us under the Lordship of Jesus. We do not obey to become our own foundation. We obey because Christ is the foundation and His words are true.

Ask honestly: where have I heard Jesus clearly but delayed obedience?

That place matters. Not because God is waiting to crush you, but because He is calling you onto solid ground. His commands are not traps. They are truth. They are life. They are foundation.

The rain will come in some form. The wind will blow. The question is not whether life will ever be tested. The question is what has been built underneath.

Do not wait for the storm to care about the ground.

Let obedience lay the foundation where Christ is Lord.
