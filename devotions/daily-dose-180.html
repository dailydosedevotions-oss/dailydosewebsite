Daily Dose #180

October 21, 2026

CHILDREN ARE A GIFT

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on October 21, 2026.

PSALM 127:3 (ESV)

"Behold, children are a heritage from the LORD, the fruit of the womb a reward."

Scripture speaks of children as a gift from the Lord.

That truth is beautiful, but it is also weighty. A gift from God is not something to possess carelessly, control selfishly, or treat as an interruption to our own importance. A child is to be received with gratitude and stewarded with humility.

Psalm 127 reminds us that life is not ultimately in our hands. Every breath is mercy. Every child points to the Creator who forms, knows, and values people before they can achieve anything, prove anything, or give anything back.

The world often measures worth by usefulness, success, independence, appearance, or ability. Scripture begins somewhere deeper. Life has value because God is the giver of it. Children are not valuable because they are easy, convenient, impressive, or able to meet expectations. They are valuable because they are made by God and entrusted by God.

This truth should shape how children are welcomed, protected, taught, corrected, and loved.

It should make us gentle with their weakness and serious about their souls. It should make us patient in training and prayerful in responsibility. It should make us careful not to treat them as burdens to escape, trophies to display, or extensions of our own pride.

Jesus showed a tenderness toward children that corrected the assumptions of those around Him. MARK 10:14 (ESV) records Him saying, "Let the children come to me; do not hinder them."

That sentence carries both welcome and warning. Let them come. Do not hinder them. Children should not be pushed away from the Lord by neglect, hypocrisy, harshness, prayerlessness, or a home where Christ is named but not honoured.

This devotion may also touch tender places.

For some, the subject of children carries longing, grief, waiting, loss, infertility, miscarriage, complicated family pain, or wounds that are not easily spoken. The Bible's goodness does not require us to ignore sorrow. We can honour the truth that children are a gift while also bringing every ache honestly before the compassionate God.

God is not careless with tender places.

Where children are present, may they be received with love, protected with wisdom, and pointed toward Christ.

Where longing remains, may hearts be held by the Lord who sees.

Where parenting feels overwhelming, may grace meet the responsibility one day at a time.

Where mistakes have been made, may repentance become real and restoration be sought.

Children are a gift, but they are not ours in the ultimate sense. They belong first to the Lord. That should make us humble, prayerful, and dependent.

That truth also protects us from making children carry burdens they were never meant to carry. They are not here to fulfil our identity, heal our insecurity, or perform a version of success that makes us feel important. They are souls to be loved, taught, corrected, protected, and pointed again and again to Christ.

GENESIS 1:27 (ESV) says that God created man in His own image. That means the worth of a child is not waiting for adulthood, achievement, personality, or usefulness. From the beginning, human life carries dignity because God is Creator. This should make us careful with our words, patient with weakness, and serious about the atmosphere we create.

PSALM 139:13 (ESV) says, "For you formed my inward parts; you knitted me together in my mother's womb." The Lord's knowledge of a person begins before people can see or celebrate them. He is near to the hidden places of life. He forms with wisdom where human eyes cannot reach.

So receive children with reverence, but do not idolise them. Love them deeply, but do not make them the centre in a way only God should be. Pray for them with faith, but remember that salvation belongs to the Lord. Teach them truth, but trust God with the work only He can do in the heart.

There is comfort in that for tired parents and for anyone who carries responsibility for young lives. You are called to faithfulness, not control. You can plant, water, teach, correct, pray, and model repentance, but only God gives life. That should not make us careless; it should make us prayerful.

3 JOHN 4 (ESV) says, "I have no greater joy than to hear that my children are walking in the truth." Whether speaking of spiritual children or those entrusted to us in family, the deepest desire is not merely that they do well outwardly, but that they walk in truth. That is why we keep pointing them to Christ.

Receive life with reverence.

Steward what God entrusts with love.
