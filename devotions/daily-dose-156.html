Daily Dose #156

September 27, 2026

THE BETTER PORTION

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on September 27, 2026.

LUKE 10:41-42 (ESV)

"But the Lord answered her, 'Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled about many things, but one thing is necessary.'"

Martha was serving, but her heart had become troubled.

That matters because service itself was not the problem. Jesus did not rebuke her because she cared about practical things. He did not say that serving had no value. The issue was that her activity had become filled with anxiety, frustration, and distraction.

Mary was sitting at the Lord's feet, listening to His word. Martha was busy with much serving. From the outside, Martha may have looked like the more responsible one. She was doing what needed to be done. She was carrying the load. She was moving, preparing, handling, managing.

But Jesus saw the heart.

He said, "Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled about many things, but one thing is necessary."

That sentence reaches into more than one home in Bethany. It reaches into every heart that has become so consumed with doing that it has stopped receiving. It reaches into every believer who is active for God but no longer still with God. It reaches into every person who is carrying many things while neglecting the one thing necessary.

This is a word for busy souls.

We can fill our days with good things and still miss the better portion. We can serve with our hands while our souls become irritated, anxious, and distant from the Lord. We can be involved in ministry, family, work, responsibility, and even good spiritual activity, while quietly becoming disconnected from the presence of Christ.

Activity can hide distance for a while.

That is what makes it dangerous. If someone is openly running from God, the problem is easier to see. But when someone is busy around holy things, it can be harder to recognise that the heart has stopped sitting at His feet.

Martha's complaint also reveals how anxiety can twist our view of people. She says, in effect, "Lord, do you not care that my sister has left me to serve alone?" When the heart is troubled, it can begin to interpret others through frustration. Mary becomes the problem. Jesus is questioned. Service becomes resentment.

That can happen to us too.

When we are serving from pressure instead of nearness, we may become sharp with the people around us. We notice who is not helping. We compare loads. We feel unseen. We may even bring our complaint to Jesus while missing the fact that He is lovingly exposing the state of our own heart.

Jesus does not shame Martha.

He calls her name twice. "Martha, Martha." There is tenderness in that. He is not dismissing her. He is inviting her to see what anxiety has done. She is troubled by many things, but one thing is necessary.

Mary chose the good portion. She placed herself where His word could be heard. She gave Him her attention. She received before she moved.

MATTHEW 11:28-29 (ESV) says, "Come to me, all who labor and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."

Notice the invitation: come to Me.

Not first, do more.

Not first, prove more.

Not first, carry more.

Come.

The better portion begins there. It begins with returning to the Lord Himself, not merely the work connected to His name.

This does not mean responsibilities disappear. Martha still had real things in front of her. We also have real responsibilities. Families need care. Work needs attention. People need serving. Duties still matter. But Jesus reorders the heart so that service flows from nearness instead of anxiety.

Service that flows from nearness has a different spirit.

It can still be tired, but it is not bitter in the same way. It can still work hard, but it is not trying to earn love. It can still carry responsibility, but it does not make responsibility into an idol. It receives from Christ before trying to pour out for others.

That is why the better portion must not be treated as laziness. Sitting at Jesus' feet is not doing nothing. It is receiving the Word of life. It is letting His voice become louder than the pressure. It is allowing His presence to correct the anxious heart before the hands continue working.

PSALM 27:4 (ESV) says, "One thing have I asked of the LORD, that will I seek after."

David's one thing and Mary's good portion both call us away from a scattered heart. Many things may matter, but they must not take the place of the Lord Himself.

Maybe you have been carrying many things. Maybe none of them are wrong in themselves, but your soul has become troubled underneath them. Maybe you have been around the things of God while not actually sitting with God.

This is not a call to abandon faithfulness. It is a call to return to the source of it.

Open the Word before the noise takes over. Pray honestly instead of rushing past God. Let Jesus have your attention, not just your effort.

The better portion is where love is restored before labour continues.
