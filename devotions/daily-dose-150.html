Daily Dose #150

September 21, 2026

KEEP WALKING IN HIM

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on September 21, 2026.

COLOSSIANS 2:6-7 (ESV)

"Therefore, as you received Christ Jesus the Lord, so walk in him, rooted and built up in him and established in the faith."

The Christian life does not begin with Jesus and then move on without Him.

Paul writes, "As therefore you received Christ Jesus the Lord, walk in him." That is simple, but it reaches deeply. The way we first came to Christ is the way we must continue in Christ. We came by faith. We came needy. We came receiving grace, not proving ourselves. We came because God opened our eyes to see that we could not save ourselves.

But after beginning that way, it is possible to start living as though everything now depends on our own strength.

We can receive Christ by faith and then try to grow by pressure. We can begin with grace and then slip into striving. We can confess that Jesus is Lord, yet quietly take back control of the areas we find hardest to surrender.

Paul does not tell believers to walk apart from Him, around Him, or merely near Him. He says to walk "in him."

That means Christ is not only the doorway into salvation. He is the ground beneath every step. He is not only where we begin. He is where we remain.

That matters because many people try to live the Christian life from memory instead of communion. They remember what God has done, they know the right truths, they understand the language of faith, but their present walk has become dry because they are no longer drawing daily life from Christ.

You cannot live today on yesterday's nearness.

The Lord gives fresh mercy, fresh strength, fresh correction, and fresh grace. Not because the gospel changes, but because we are continually dependent on the same Christ who saved us.

Paul says we are to be "rooted and built up in him." Roots go downward. Buildings rise upward. A healthy Christian life has both depth and growth. There is hidden work beneath the surface, and there is visible fruit above it.

We often want the visible fruit quickly. We want stronger faith. We want better habits. We want clearer direction. We want to feel more mature. We want old struggles gone. We want the outside to show progress immediately.

But roots take time.

Foundations are not glamorous, but without them, what is built cannot stand. Much of what God does in a person happens in hidden places before it becomes visible to others. He strengthens convictions. He exposes motives. He teaches patience. He corrects false confidence. He deepens dependence. He forms obedience when nobody is watching.

This is why continuing with Jesus matters.

You do not become rooted by visiting Him occasionally. You become rooted by remaining. By returning to His Word. By staying in prayer. By obeying when it costs. By repenting when you drift. By giving thanks when the season is not easy. By allowing Scripture to have authority over your feelings, not just your opinions.

HEBREWS 12:2 (ESV) tells us to look "to Jesus, the founder and perfecter of our faith."

That is not a suggestion for especially spiritual people. It is the normal Christian life.

Branches do not produce fruit by detaching themselves and trying harder. They bear fruit because they remain connected to the vine. The life of the vine flows into the branch. The fruit does not come from the branch pretending to be strong on its own.

This challenges our self-reliance.

There are times we try to fix ourselves apart from Christ. We make plans, set goals, put pressure on ourselves, compare ourselves to others, and wonder why we still feel dry inside. But spiritual growth is not produced by human pressure alone. It comes from life in Christ.

That does not mean discipline is unnecessary. Scripture calls us to obedience, endurance, prayer, self-control, and action. But Christian discipline is meant to flow from dependence, not replace it.

If discipline becomes disconnected from Christ, it can turn into pride when we succeed and despair when we fail. But when discipline flows from remaining in Him, it becomes a way of walking with the One who is already our life.

Paul also says we are to be "established in the faith."

To be established is to be strengthened and settled in truth. Not tossed around by every feeling. Not moved by every opinion. Not constantly rebuilding the foundation because emotions have shifted. The believer is meant to grow more steady in what God has said.

That steadiness matters because life will test what we are established in.

If we are established in feelings, we will be unstable when feelings change. If we are established in people's approval, we will be shaken when people misunderstand us. If we are established in circumstances, we will lose peace every time the situation changes.

But if we are established in Christ, we may still feel the shaking, but we are not built on what is shaking.

Paul ends with thanksgiving: "abounding in it in thanksgiving."

Thanksgiving protects the heart from pride and forgetfulness. A thankful person remembers that everything good has come from God. Gratitude keeps us from acting as though we built ourselves. It reminds us that we are recipients before we are workers, branches before we are fruit-bearers, children before we are servants.

Maybe today the question is not whether you started with Christ.

Maybe the question is whether you are still walking in Him.

Have you slowly shifted from dependence into pressure? Have you been trying to carry spiritually what only Christ can sustain? Have you been doing Christian things while your heart has been running on empty?

The answer is not to abandon obedience. The answer is to return to the right source.

Keep walking in Him.

Not ahead of Him.

Not away from Him.

Not merely around Christian activity.

In Him.

The One who saved you is the One who sustains you. The One who called you is the One who builds you. The One who began the work is faithful to continue it.

Walk today from the life you have received, not from the strength you are trying to manufacture.
