No Condemnation in Christ

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on February 9, 2027.

ROMANS 8:1 (ESV)

"There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus."

Condemnation is heavier than conviction. Conviction exposes sin so we can turn to God. Condemnation declares there is no hope. ROMANS 8:1 (ESV) says, "There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus."

That word now matters. Not after years of proving yourself. Not after you feel worthy. Not after you punish yourself enough. Now, for those who are in Christ Jesus, there is no condemnation. The believer is not standing before God under the final sentence of guilt. Christ has borne what sin deserved.

This is not a small doctrine. If we misunderstand it, we will either treat sin lightly or live crushed under accusations. Grace does neither. Grace tells the truth about sin and then points to the finished work of Christ. No condemnation does not mean no correction. It means correction comes from a Father, not a Judge preparing to destroy those who are in His Son.

JOHN 5:24 (ESV) says the one who hears Christ's word and believes "does not come into judgment, but has passed from death to life." That is the security of the gospel.

When conviction comes, repent quickly. When accusation comes, answer with Christ. The cross is stronger than your shame. In Him, there is no condemnation.

This freedom should produce holiness, not carelessness. A condemned heart hides, performs, or gives up. A forgiven heart can come into the light and be changed. Because Christ has removed condemnation, sin no longer needs to be protected by secrecy. You can confess, repent, and rise again under grace. The verdict over those in Christ is not hanging by a thread. No condemnation means the believer can fight sin from acceptance, not for acceptance.

So do not let accusation do what only the Holy Spirit should do. The Spirit convicts and leads to Christ. The accuser crushes and drives into despair. Learn the difference, and answer condemnation with the gospel.
