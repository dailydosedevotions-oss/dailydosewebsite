Be Holy in All Your Conduct

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on January 14, 2027.

1 PETER 1:15-16 (ESV)

"As he who called you is holy, you also be holy in all your conduct."

Holiness is not a harsh word when understood rightly. 1 PETER 1:15-16 (ESV) calls believers to be holy because the One who called them is holy. Holiness means belonging to God. It means being set apart for Him in conduct, desire, speech, and worship.

The world often treats holiness as narrow, joyless, or outdated. The flesh treats holiness as an interruption. But Scripture treats holiness as fitting for those who have been called by the holy God. We cannot claim to belong to Him while making peace with what dishonours Him.

Peter says "in all your conduct." That reaches beyond public image. It reaches private habits, hidden motives, relationships, online choices, entertainment, money, conflict, and the way we respond when no one is watching. Holiness is not a costume for religious settings. It is the shape of a life increasingly surrendered to God.

1 THESSALONIANS 4:7 (ESV) says, "For God has not called us for impurity, but in holiness." That calling is grace. God does not save us so we can remain comfortable in sin. He calls us into the life that belongs to Him.

Do not reduce holiness to appearance. Ask the Lord to make you holy where it is costly and unseen. The One who called you is holy, and His grace trains His people to live as His own.

Holiness also grows through daily choices, not only major moments. The heart is trained by what it repeatedly allows and repeatedly refuses. Small compromises can dull us, and small obediences can strengthen us. This is why conduct matters. The way we speak when annoyed, the way we respond to temptation, the way we handle privacy, and the way we treat people when tired all belong to the Lord. Holiness reaches the whole life because God has claimed the whole person.
