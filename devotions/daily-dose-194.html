Quiet Trust in the Waiting: Part 1

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on November 4, 2026.

PSALM 62:5-8 (ESV)

"For God alone, O my soul, wait in silence, for my hope is from him."

Quiet trust is not easy when the mind is loud.

There are times when the body is still, but the thoughts keep moving. The outside may look calm, but inside there can be a rush of questions, fears, calculations, conversations, and possible outcomes. The mind tries to solve what the heart is afraid to surrender.

Scripture does not ignore that kind of struggle. In PSALM 62:5 (ESV), David says, "For God alone, O my soul, wait in silence, for my hope is from him." Notice what is happening. David is speaking to his own soul. He is not pretending quietness comes naturally. He is directing his inner life back toward God.

That is important because biblical quietness is not emptiness. It is not switching off thought as though trust means having no concern. It is the soul learning to come under the truth of who God is.

David continues in PSALM 62:6 (ESV), "He only is my rock and my salvation, my fortress; I shall not be shaken." The quietness of Psalm 62 is built on God Himself. The soul can wait because God is rock. God is salvation. God is fortress. The believer is not being asked to rest on a feeling, a guess, or a wish.

This speaks directly to the human mind.

When the mind is unsettled, it often wants something visible to hold. A message. A sign. A clear answer. A guarantee that the waiting will end exactly as desired. But David does not anchor hope in the timing of the answer. He says, "my hope is from him."

The Lord Himself is the source of hope.

That means quiet trust is not the same as pretending the situation does not matter. It means the situation is not allowed to become lord over the soul. The waiting may be real, but it is not greater than God.

PSALM 94:19 (ESV) says, "When the cares of my heart are many, your consolations cheer my soul." God does not despise the heart that has many cares. He gives comfort strong enough to reach the inner life.

Sometimes the most faithful thing you can do is speak Scripture to your own soul. Not dramatically. Not to impress anyone. Simply and honestly. "God is my refuge." "My hope is from Him." "He is my rock." "He has not left me."

Quietness may begin as a fight.

It may mean refusing to rehearse fear for the tenth time. It may mean turning off noise long enough to hear the Word. It may mean choosing not to answer from panic. It may mean sitting before God without demanding that He explain everything first.

Psalm 62 does not call you to trust your ability to become calm.

It calls you to trust God.

And when the mind is loud, the soul can still be led back, one truth at a time, to the Lord who is refuge.
