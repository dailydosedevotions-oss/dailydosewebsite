Daily Dose #196

November 6, 2026

Quiet Trust in the Waiting: Part 3

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on November 6, 2026.

HABAKKUK 3:17-19 (ESV)

"Yet I will rejoice in the LORD; I will take joy in the God of my salvation."

There is a kind of trust that is revealed when nothing visible has changed yet.

Habakkuk does not end his book with easy circumstances. He speaks of fig trees without blossom, vines without fruit, fields without yield, flocks cut off, and stalls empty. These are not small inconveniences. They are pictures of loss, lack, and visible barrenness.

Then comes the word that makes the passage so powerful: "yet."

In HABAKKUK 3:18 (ESV), the prophet says, "yet I will rejoice in the LORD; I will take joy in the God of my salvation." The joy is not rooted in full barns. It is rooted in the Lord. Habakkuk is not rejoicing because the hardship is pleasant. He is rejoicing because God is still God when the fields are empty.

That is not natural to the human heart.

When we are waiting, we often measure God's faithfulness by what has visibly improved. If the answer comes, we feel steady. If the provision appears, we breathe easier. If the door opens, we praise. But Habakkuk shows a deeper kind of trust, one that worships before the outward scene has changed.

This does not make pain unreal. Biblical faith is not pretending empty places are full. Habakkuk names the emptiness honestly. The fig tree is not blossoming. The herd is not in the stall. Faith is not denial. Faith is confidence in God in the presence of facts that still hurt.

That is why HABAKKUK 3:19 (ESV) matters: "GOD, the Lord, is my strength." The prophet's strength is not drawn from the field, the flock, the harvest, or the visible outcome. His strength is the Lord Himself.

There are seasons when God strengthens His people before He changes what they can see.

That can be hard to receive. We often want the answer more than endurance. We want the burden removed more than strength under it. But the Lord knows how to sustain His people in ways that prove He is enough, even while the waiting remains.

2 CORINTHIANS 5:7 (ESV) says, "for we walk by faith, not by sight." That does not mean sight is irrelevant. It means sight is not final. What you can see today is not the whole story, and it is not the measure of God's power, wisdom, or love.

Habakkuk teaches us to say, "Yet." Not as a slogan. Not as pressure to sound spiritual. As worship. As surrender. As a declaration that the Lord remains worthy before the harvest appears.

The empty field may be real.

But so is the God of your salvation.

Quiet trust reaches maturity when the soul can say, even here, even now, even before I understand it all, my strength is in the Lord.
