Daily Dose #149

September 20, 2026

PLANTED WHERE GOD SUSTAINS

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on September 20, 2026.

JEREMIAH 17:7-8 (ESV)

"Blessed is the man who trusts in the LORD, whose trust is the LORD. He is like a tree planted by water, that sends out its roots by the stream, and does not fear when heat comes, for its leaves remain green, and is not anxious in the year of drought, for it does not cease to bear fruit."

There is a strength that does not come from life becoming easier.

Jeremiah gives us the picture of a tree planted by water. It is not strong because the weather is always gentle. It is not fruitful because the heat never comes. It is not steady because drought never touches the land. The heat still comes. The dry season still comes. The pressure is still real.

The difference is where the roots are.

That is what trust in the Lord does in a person's life. It does not mean we never face trouble. It does not mean we never feel tired, stretched, disappointed, or uncertain. It means our lives are no longer drawing from the surface as the final source of strength. We are not trying to survive on the weather. We are rooted in God.

Jeremiah says, "Blessed is the man who trusts in the LORD, whose trust is the LORD."

That word confidence matters.

Many people say they trust God, but pressure has a way of revealing where confidence really sits. When everything feels steady, it can be easy to say the right thing. It can be easy to speak faith, quote Scripture, and believe we are fully resting in the Lord. But when the heat comes, the roots begin to show.

If our confidence is in control, we panic when control slips. If our confidence is in people, we fall apart when they change, leave, misunderstand, or disappoint us. If our confidence is in money, we lose peace when provision feels uncertain. If our confidence is in routine, we become shaken when life is interrupted. If our confidence is in our own strength, we become discouraged when we reach the end of it.

The heat does not always create the problem. Sometimes it reveals what we were already depending on.

That is not said to condemn us. It is mercy when God shows us that our roots have been drinking from something that cannot sustain us. The Lord loves His people enough to call them back from false confidence before it collapses underneath them.

A tree planted by water has a hidden life. People may see the leaves, the shade, the fruit, and the growth, but they do not see the roots reaching down. Yet the hidden part is what makes the visible part possible.

The same is true spiritually.

There is a hidden life with God that cannot be replaced by appearances. It is possible to look active, busy, and put together while the soul is drying out beneath the surface. It is possible to keep serving, keep speaking, keep showing up, and still be drawing from the wrong place.

Jesus said in JOHN 15:4-5 (ESV), "Abide in me, and I in you... I am the vine; you are the branches."

Apart from Him, we can still do things. We can still stay busy. We can still appear productive. But Jesus says we cannot bear the fruit He is calling us to bear apart from Him. Real spiritual fruit comes from remaining. It comes from life flowing from Christ into the person who stays connected to Him.

Psalm 1 gives a similar picture. PSALM 1:2-3 (ESV) says the one who delights in the law of the Lord "is like a tree planted by streams of water that yields its fruit in its season."

Notice that fruit comes "in its season."

That is important because sometimes we expect fruit immediately. We want God to show results quickly. We want the growth to be obvious. We want the answer, the breakthrough, the change, the open door, the visible evidence that what we are doing matters.

But trees do not panic because fruit is seasonal.

They remain planted.

There are seasons where God is deepening roots before He shows fruit. There are seasons where He is teaching trust before He changes circumstances. There are seasons where He is forming endurance, humility, patience, and dependence in places nobody else can see.

Do not mistake hidden work for wasted time.

Jeremiah says this tree "will not fear when heat comes." It does not say the heat will never come. It says the tree will not be ruled by fear when it does. Why? Because its life is not depending on the heat leaving. Its life is drawing from the water beneath.

Trusting God does not make a person numb. You may feel concern, grief, tiredness, uncertainty, or heaviness. The Bible is honest about human weakness. But there is a difference between feeling the heat and being ruled by it.

A rooted person may feel pressure, but pressure does not become lord. A rooted person may walk through drought, but drought does not become identity. A rooted person may not understand the season, but the season does not become greater than God.

Where are your roots?

Not where do you say they are when everything is calm. Where do they reach when pressure comes? What do you run to first? What do you believe you cannot be okay without? What loss would make you feel as though God was no longer enough?

Those questions can search us deeply.

And if the answer reveals misplaced confidence, bring it honestly before the Lord. Do not dress it up. Do not pretend. God is not asking for a polished version of your heart. He is calling you back to Himself.

To trust in the Lord is not to say circumstances do not matter. It is to say circumstances do not have the final word.

There may be heat in your life right now. There may be a dry place you did not expect. But this does not have to be the place where you wither. It can become the place where your roots go deeper.

The Lord is able to sustain what the season cannot support.

Let the drought expose the false sources, but do not let it steal your hope. The water is still there. The Lord is still faithful. Let the roots go down again.
