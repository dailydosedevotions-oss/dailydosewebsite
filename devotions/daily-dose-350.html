Abide in Christ, Part 2: The Father Prunes

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on April 9, 2027.

JOHN 15:2 (ESV)

“Every branch that does bear fruit he prunes, that it may bear more fruit.”

Pruning can look like loss. A healthy branch is cut back, not because the gardener hates it, but because he knows what will help it grow.

Jesus said His Father prunes fruitful branches. That means difficulty is not always evidence that God has abandoned someone. At times, He removes habits, ambitions or attachments that are limiting healthier growth. He may expose pride, interrupt a comfortable routine or close a door we were determined to enter.

Not every painful event should be labelled as God’s pruning, and suffering must never be explained carelessly. Scripture gives us room to grieve and ask questions. Yet it also teaches that God can use hardship, correction and disappointment to shape those who belong to Him.

Pruning reveals what we have been leaning on. When recognition disappears, do we still obey? When plans change, do we still trust? When God’s Word confronts us, do we defend ourselves or allow Him to cut away what is unhealthy?

The Father’s purpose is not to make His people smaller. It is to make their lives more fruitful and more like Christ. What feels like reduction may be preparation.

Hebrews 12 uses another picture: a loving father disciplines his children for their good. Both pictures remind us that God’s correction has a purpose. Yet we should be cautious about announcing why another person is suffering. We do not know every purpose of God, and careless explanations can add pain. The first question is not, “What is God cutting out of them?” but, “Lord, what are You teaching me, and where do I need to respond?”

Ask God for the humility to release what He is removing and the faith to trust what He is producing.
