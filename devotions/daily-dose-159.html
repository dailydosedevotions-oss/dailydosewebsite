Daily Dose #159

September 30, 2026

HELD TO THE PROMISE

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on September 30, 2026.

HEBREWS 10:23 (ESV)

"Let us hold fast the confession of our hope without wavering; for he who promised is faithful."

The call to hold fast is not based on the strength of our grip alone.

Hebrews gives the reason: "for he who promised is faithful." Our hope rests on God's character, not our mood. That is good news, because moods change. Emotions rise and fall. Confidence can feel strong one day and fragile the next. But God does not become faithful only when we feel steady.

He is faithful because faithfulness belongs to who He is.

There are days when faith feels clear. You can pray with confidence, read Scripture with hunger, and feel settled in what God has spoken. There are other days when everything in you feels pulled in different directions. You may know the truth and still feel pressure. You may believe God and still feel tired. You may hold hope and still feel the weight of waiting.

The command is not to pretend we never feel pressure. It is to hold fast because the One who promised has not changed.

This verse does not point us to wishful thinking. It does not tell us to build confidence on whatever we hope will happen. It points us to the faithfulness of God revealed through Christ. God keeps His word. He finishes what He begins. He remains true when our emotions rise and fall.

NUMBERS 23:19 (ESV) says, "God is not man, that he should lie, or a son of man, that he should change his mind."

God's promises are not fragile because God is not false. He does not speak carelessly. He does not forget what He has said. He is not limited by human weakness or changed by human instability.

That matters when waiting stretches longer than expected.

Sometimes delay begins to question what God has spoken. Sometimes disappointment makes the heart cautious. Sometimes we become afraid to hope because hope has hurt before. But biblical hope is not fragile optimism resting on ideal circumstances. It is confidence anchored in the faithful God.

LAMENTATIONS 3:22-23 (ESV) says, "The steadfast love of the LORD never ceases; his mercies never come to an end."

Those words were not written from an easy place. They rise from sorrow and devastation, yet they declare the faithfulness of God. That teaches us something important. Faithfulness is not only confessed when life is comfortable. Sometimes it is confessed when everything around us says grief should get the final word.

Holding fast does not mean we never have questions.

Many faithful people in Scripture cried out to God with honest sorrow, confusion, and longing. The Psalms are full of prayers that do not pretend. But honest lament is different from letting go of hope. The grieving believer can still cling to the faithful God.

HEBREWS 6:19 (ESV) says, "We have this as a sure and steadfast anchor of the soul."

An anchor does not stop the waves from moving. It holds the vessel when the waves are real. Hope in Christ is like that. It does not mean the sea is calm every day. It means the soul is held by something stronger than the storm.

That is why the command says to hold fast "without wavering." Wavering is not the same as feeling weak. A believer can feel weak and still hold fast. Wavering is what happens when the heart begins to loosen its grip on what God has said and starts treating something else as more reliable.

It may be fear.

It may be control.

It may be disappointment.

It may be the voice of someone who does not know the Lord.

It may be the pressure of a long wait.

The writer of Hebrews is calling weary believers not to let go of their confession. That confession is not vague positivity. It is the hope we have in Christ: His finished work, His priestly intercession, His promised return, His keeping grace, and His faithfulness to bring His people home.

HEBREWS 4:14 (ESV) says, "Let us hold fast our confession." We do not hold fast because we are impressive. We hold fast because Christ is sufficient.

Hold fast.

Not to the idea that life will always go the way you imagined.

Not to a timeline God never promised.

Not to control.

Hold fast to the confession of your hope in Christ.

He is risen. He is Lord. He intercedes for His people. He will finish His saving work. He will not abandon those who belong to Him.

As this month closes, do not measure your hope by how strong you feel. Look again at the One who promised.

Hope does not have to be loud to be real. Sometimes it is simply the soul refusing to let go because God is faithful.
