Daily Dose #164

October 5, 2026

CLEAN HANDS, A PURE HEART

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on October 5, 2026.

PSALM 24:3-4 (ESV)

"Who shall ascend the hill of the LORD? And who shall stand in his holy place? He who has clean hands and a pure heart."

Psalm 24 asks a serious question: who may stand before the holy God?

The answer reaches beyond outward religion. "Clean hands" matter, but so does "a pure heart." Hands speak of what we do. The heart speaks of what we desire, love, worship, and hide. God lays claim to both.

It is possible to focus on the hands while neglecting the heart. A person can do visible things that appear righteous while motives remain selfish. A person can avoid certain outward sins while inward pride, envy, lust, bitterness, or deceit remain protected. But the Lord is not fooled by a polished surface.

He sees the whole person.

That should humble us deeply. None of us can cleanse ourselves enough to stand before God by our own righteousness. If Psalm 24 only left us with the demand, we would be undone. The holiness of God exposes us. We need mercy. We need cleansing. We need more than improvement.

We need grace.

1 JOHN 1:9 (ESV) says, "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins."

That is the mercy of God. He does not call us to pretend we are clean when we are not. He calls us to confess. He is faithful and righteous to forgive and cleanse. Forgiveness is not found in hiding sin well. It is found in bringing sin into the light before the God who cleanses through Christ.

But grace does not make holiness unimportant.

Sometimes people speak about grace as though it means God no longer cares about clean hands or pure hearts. That is not biblical grace. The grace of God does not simply cover sin so we can keep loving it. Grace forgives, cleanses, teaches, and transforms.

TITUS 2:11-12 (ESV) says, "For the grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation for all people, training us to renounce ungodliness."

Grace teaches us to deny what once ruled us. Grace trains us to live differently. Grace does not lower the holiness of God. It brings us into a life where holiness becomes possible because God is at work in us.

So Psalm 24 should not make us perform. It should make us honest.

What are your hands touching that they should not be touching? What are your actions showing? What patterns have you excused because they are familiar? What has your heart been lifting itself toward? Where has falsehood become easier than truth?

These are not questions for condemnation. They are questions for surrender.

The Lord is not asking for a life that merely looks clean from a distance. He is calling for the inner and outer life to be brought before Him. Not so you can earn your way into His presence, but because His presence is holy and His grace is powerful enough to cleanse what sin has stained.

This is where confession becomes a gift. Confession is not God dragging shame out of us so we can be humiliated. Confession is agreeing with God about what is true so that sin no longer has the protection of secrecy. The hidden thing loses power when it is brought honestly before the Lord.

PSALM 32:5 (ESV) says, "I acknowledged my sin to you, and I did not cover my iniquity."

David found mercy not by covering sin, but by confessing it. That is still the way of freedom. Clean hands and a pure heart are not produced by pretending. They are formed as the holy God cleanses, forgives, corrects, and teaches us to walk in the light.

The invitation is serious, but it is also hopeful because Christ is sufficient for sinners who come honestly.

This also guards us from performing holiness for people while resisting holiness before God. It is possible to care more about being seen as clean than actually being clean. It is possible to protect reputation while neglecting repentance. But the Lord is not calling us to image management. He is calling us into truth.

MATTHEW 5:8 (ESV) says, "Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God."

Purity of heart matters because God Himself matters. The goal is not merely to avoid consequences. The goal is to belong wholly to Him.

Do not hide behind religious activity.

Do not settle for clean-looking hands while the heart remains divided.

Come honestly to the God who sees both.
