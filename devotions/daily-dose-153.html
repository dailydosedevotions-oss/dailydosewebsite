Daily Dose #153

September 24, 2026

WHEN OBEDIENCE FEELS SMALL

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on September 24, 2026.

LUKE 16:10 (ESV)

"One who is faithful in a very little is also faithful in much."

Some obedience does not feel significant while it is happening.

A quiet apology. A hidden act of service. Telling the truth when a lie would be easier. Choosing patience when irritation rises. Praying again when nothing seems to be changing. Showing up faithfully when nobody claps.

These moments may not look large, but they matter before God.

Jesus says, "One who is faithful in a very little is also faithful in much."

That means the small places are not throwaway places. They reveal the heart. They train the heart. They show what we truly value when there is no audience and no immediate reward.

We often imagine faithfulness as something dramatic. We think of big assignments, visible callings, major decisions, or moments where everything is clearly on the line. But much of the Christian life is formed in ordinary obedience.

The way you speak when you are tired matters.

The way you handle responsibility when it feels repetitive matters.

The way you treat people who cannot give you anything back matters.

The way you obey God when nobody else would know the difference matters.

Small does not mean unseen.

Scripture says in 1 CORINTHIANS 10:31 (ESV), "whatever you do, do all to the glory of God."

That changes the weight of ordinary things. If something can be done unto the Lord, then it is not meaningless. The task may be small, but the One you are serving is not.

This is where many hearts are tested. We may be willing to obey when the assignment feels important, but careless when it feels hidden. We may be diligent when someone is watching, but lazy when no one will notice. We may say we want to serve God greatly while neglecting the quiet obedience already in front of us.

But faithfulness does not begin later.

It begins here.

If God has given you a small responsibility, steward it well. If He has given you a hidden assignment, honour Him in it. If He is asking for obedience in something nobody else sees, do not treat it as unimportant.

HEBREWS 6:10 (ESV) says that God is not unjust so as to overlook your work and the love shown for His name.

God does not miss what people overlook. He does not need a crowd to notice obedience. He sees the heart behind the action. He sees the choice nobody else knew was costly. He sees the restraint. He sees the quiet prayer. He sees the unseen service. He sees the small yes that was actually a deep surrender.

Small obedience can also protect us from larger compromise.

If we keep dismissing little convictions, the heart becomes dull. If we keep saying yes to God in little places, the heart becomes trained in surrender. A person does not usually drift into major unfaithfulness all at once. Often, the heart first learns to ignore the small warnings.

That is why little things matter.

The hidden yes matters.

The quiet refusal matters.

The small act of obedience matters.

1 CORINTHIANS 1:28 (ESV) says God chose what is low and despised in the world, even things that are not, to bring to nothing things that are.

God is not embarrassed by small beginnings, hidden obedience, or quiet faithfulness. People may despise what looks small because people often measure by size, speed, applause, and visibility. But God sees differently. He knows what He is forming in hidden places.

Maybe the thing in front of you today does not feel impressive. Maybe it is a responsibility you have carried for a long time. Maybe it is an apology you need to make. Maybe it is a habit you need to stop feeding. Maybe it is a task nobody thanks you for. Maybe it is choosing integrity when compromise would be easier.

Do not underestimate the place God has you.

The room may be ordinary. The assignment may feel repetitive. The step may seem small. But if the Lord is asking for faithfulness there, then there is nothing small about obeying Him.

This also protects the heart from living for applause.

If we only obey when people notice, then people have become too important in our obedience. If we only serve when there is recognition, then recognition has become part of the reward we are chasing. Jesus frees us from that. He teaches us to live before the Father.

That does not mean encouragement from people is wrong. It can be a gift. But it cannot become the reason we obey. The servant of the Lord must be able to continue when nobody sees, nobody thanks, and nobody understands the cost.

There is a steadiness that forms when obedience becomes about God first.

That kind of faithfulness may not look dramatic, but over time it builds a life. One truthful choice after another. One surrendered response after another. One hidden act of service after another. One quiet refusal of compromise after another. The life becomes shaped by repeated obedience in places the world may never celebrate.

2 THESSALONIANS 3:13 (ESV) says, "As for you, brothers, do not grow weary in doing good."

That verse matters because small obedience can become tiring when fruit is slow. Doing good without immediate results can test the heart. But Scripture tells us not to grow weary. There is a due season known to God. The harvest belongs to Him.

Heaven takes seriously what pride calls insignificant.
