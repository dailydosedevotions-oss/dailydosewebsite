Daily Dose #187

October 28, 2026

A NEW HEART

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on October 28, 2026.

EZEKIEL 36:26-27 (ESV)

"And I will give you a new heart, and a new spirit I will put within you."

God's promise through Ezekiel reaches deeper than outward improvement.

He promises a new heart and a new spirit. We do not merely need better behaviour placed on top of the same old rebellion. We need inward transformation.

That is humbling because it means our problem is deeper than habits alone. Bad fruit comes from a deeper root. Sin is not only something we do; it is something that has affected the heart. We need more than advice, motivation, image management, or religious appearance.

We need God to do what only God can do.

The Lord says He will remove the heart of stone and give a heart of flesh. A stone heart is hard, resistant, unresponsive, and cold toward God. A heart of flesh is living, tender, responsive, and able to be shaped by Him.

This is the mercy of God.

He does not only command His people from a distance and leave them powerless. He gives what He requires. He works inwardly. He puts His Spirit within His people and causes them to walk in His statutes.

That does not make obedience unnecessary. It makes obedience possible.

2 CORINTHIANS 5:17 (ESV) says, "Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation."

The gospel is not cosmetic. In Christ, God makes people new. The old life no longer has the final claim. The patterns that once ruled us may still need to be fought, but they are no longer our identity or master.

This gives hope for the hard places.

No heart is too hard for God to transform. No person is beyond His reach because their resistance feels strong. No hidden place is beyond His ability to renew. The Lord can soften what sin has hardened. He can awaken what has gone numb. He can make obedience desirable where rebellion once felt normal.

But we should not treat this promise lightly.

If God gives a new heart, then we should not keep defending the old ways. If He puts His Spirit within us, then we should not make peace with what grieves Him. If He has made us new in Christ, then we are called to walk as people who belong to that new life.

Bring Him the places that still feel resistant.

Bring Him the coldness.

Bring Him the stubbornness.

Bring Him the places where you have said, "This is just how I am."

The God who commands obedience is also the God who gives new life.

JEREMIAH 24:7 (ESV) says, "I will give them a heart to know that I am the LORD." That promise understands that the heart needs God's creative mercy. The Lord is not offering a religious mask. He is promising inward renewal.

This is where the gospel gives hope beyond self-improvement. You may improve habits for a while and still remain unchanged at the root. You may change outward behaviour because of pressure, fear, reputation, or consequences. But only God can create a clean heart. Only God can make a person new from the inside.

TITUS 3:5 (ESV) says God saved us "by the washing of regeneration and renewal of the Holy Spirit." Salvation is not earned by our works. It is mercy. The renewal of the Spirit is not a small addition to the Christian life; it is essential to it.

So do not settle for surface change when God offers new life. Do not only ask Him to adjust what people can see. Ask Him to renew what He sees. Ask Him to soften what has been hard, cleanse what has been stained, and make obedience flow from a heart that is truly His.

A new heart also changes what we begin to love. The Lord does not merely restrain old desires; He forms new ones. Things that once seemed harmless begin to grieve us. Truth that once felt heavy begins to become beautiful. Obedience that once felt impossible begins to become the path of life.

This work may be gradual, but it is real. Do not despise the evidence of grace because growth is still unfinished. If you are more tender to conviction than you used to be, that is mercy. If you are quicker to repent, that is mercy. If you now hate what once owned you, that is mercy.

The heart of stone does not soften itself. Give thanks to the God who makes hearts alive.
