A Kingdom Without End

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on December 20, 2026.

LUKE 1:32-33 (ESV)

"Of his kingdom there will be no end."

Everything earthly has an expiry. Leaders change. Nations shift. Plans fade. Strength weakens. Trends pass. Homes age. Bodies decline. Even the things that feel stable are not ultimate. But the angel tells Mary in LUKE 1:33 (ESV) that of Christ's kingdom "there will be no end." That truth gives December a deeper hope than sentiment can provide. We are not merely remembering a beautiful birth story. We are looking to the King whose reign is everlasting. Jesus is given the throne of David. God's promises are not forgotten. The kingdom promised through Scripture finds its fulfilment in Him. He reigns not as a temporary ruler, but as the Son of the Most High. This confronts our attachments. We often live as though temporary kingdoms are ultimate. We build our security around finances, reputation, family arrangements, political hopes, personal plans, comfort, or control. Some of these may be good gifts in their proper place, but none can bear the weight of forever. Christ's kingdom can.

DANIEL 7:14 (ESV) speaks of a dominion that shall not pass away and a kingdom that shall not be destroyed. The kingdom of Christ is not fragile. It does not depend on human approval to remain true. That should make believers steady. When the world shakes, Christ's reign does not. When earthly leaders disappoint, Christ does not. When your own plans unravel, the kingdom of God has not weakened. This does not make daily responsibilities meaningless. It gives them their right place. We live faithfully now because we belong to an eternal King. Do not give your highest hope to what cannot last. Christ's kingdom has no end. Let that truth teach your heart what is worth seeking first. This is not only future comfort; it changes today. If His kingdom has no end, then obedience done for Him is not wasted. Sacrifice made for Him is not foolish. Faithfulness that no one notices is not small in the eyes of the King. 1 THESSALONIANS 2:12 (ESV) speaks of walking "in a manner worthy of God, who calls you into his own kingdom and glory." That means the soul does not have to be ruled by every passing thing. We can live steadily because our King and His kingdom endure.
