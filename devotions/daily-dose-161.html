Daily Dose #161

October 2, 2026

WORDS THAT GIVE GRACE

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on October 2, 2026.

EPHESIANS 4:29 (ESV)

"Let no corrupt speech proceed out of your mouth, but only what is good for building up as the need may be, that it may give grace to those who hear."

Words are never as small as they seem.

They can build, wound, strengthen, confuse, encourage, correct, comfort, or tear down. A sentence spoken carelessly can remain in someone's mind long after the conversation has ended. A word spoken with wisdom can strengthen someone at the exact moment they were close to giving up.

Paul tells believers, "Let no corrupt speech proceed out of your mouth."

That is strong language. He does not treat speech as harmless. Corrupt speech is not only obviously filthy talk. It can include words that rot what is good. Words that tear people down. Words that feed bitterness. Words that twist truth. Words that flatter when correction is needed. Words that mock, belittle, accuse falsely, or stir division.

The mouth of a believer is not meant to become a place where corruption flows freely.

But Paul does not only tell us what to put off. He tells us what should replace it: "only what is good for building up as the need may be, that it may give grace to those who hear."

That phrase "as the need may be" matters. Wise speech considers the moment. It asks what is needed, not only what is true. Truth spoken without love can wound unnecessarily. Love without truth can become cowardice. Grace-filled speech carries truth in a way that seeks the good of the hearer before God.

This does not mean every word must be soft or easy. Sometimes love speaks directly. Sometimes correction is necessary. Sometimes silence would be disobedience. But even correction can be gracious when it is humble, truthful, and aimed at restoration rather than humiliation.

PROVERBS 16:24 (ESV) says, "Gracious words are like a honeycomb, sweetness to the soul and health to the body."

Grace and wisdom belong together. We need the Spirit of God to govern not only whether we speak, but how we speak, when we speak, and why we speak.

Many of us have learned to excuse careless speech because we were stressed, tired, hurt, or angry. We say things like, "I was only being honest," when really we were being harsh. Or we say, "That is just how I am," as though a sinful pattern should be protected instead of brought under Christ.

But the gospel reaches the mouth.

Jesus said in LUKE 6:45 (ESV), "Out of the abundance of the heart his mouth speaks."

If corrupt words keep coming out, God may be revealing something deeper than a speech problem. He may be showing what has been stored in the heart.

That is mercy.

Instead of only trying to control the tongue in the moment, bring the source to the Lord. Ask Him to deal with the bitterness, pride, fear, insecurity, jealousy, or anger that keeps finding a way into your words.

Your speech can become different. Not by pretending, but by being renewed.

Imagine if the people closest to you began to hear more grace from you. Not flattery. Not avoidance. Grace. Words that build what is good. Words that fit the need. Words that help carry truth without crushing. Words that make room for repentance, healing, courage, and hope.

Your mouth can become a place where the work of Christ is heard.

This will require more than trying to sound nicer. It requires the heart to be brought under the rule of Christ. If the heart is full of bitterness, the mouth will eventually leak bitterness. If the heart is full of pride, words will often carry superiority. If the heart is full of fear, speech may become controlling. So the prayer is not only, "Lord, change my words," but also, "Lord, change what my words are flowing from."

PSALM 141:3 (ESV) says, "Set a guard, O LORD, over my mouth; keep watch over the door of my lips!"

That is a prayer of dependence. It admits that the mouth needs guarding. It admits that we cannot always trust the first thing we feel like saying. It brings speech under the watch of God.

There may be a conversation today where grace needs to govern your tone. There may be a message you should rewrite before sending. There may be a person who needs truth, but truth carried with tenderness instead of frustration. There may be an apology that needs to happen because your words did not give grace when they should have.

Let no corrupt speech proceed unchecked.

Let grace have the next word.
