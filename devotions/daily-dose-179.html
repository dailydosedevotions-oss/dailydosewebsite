Daily Dose #179

October 20, 2026

TEACH THEM DILIGENTLY

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on October 20, 2026.

DEUTERONOMY 6:6-7 (ESV)

"And these words that I command you today shall be on your heart. You shall teach them diligently to your children."

Before God's Word is taught in the home, it is first meant to be on the heart.

That order matters. Deuteronomy does not begin with technique, presentation, or the perfect way to explain everything. It begins with the Word of God living in the heart of the one who is teaching. "These words... shall be on your heart."

We cannot faithfully pass on what we are personally treating as optional.

Children learn from what is said, but they also learn from what is seen. They notice what is valued. They notice what is repeated. They notice how quickly repentance happens after wrong has been done. They notice whether prayer is treated like a last resort or a real part of life. They notice whether Scripture has authority in ordinary decisions or only appears in religious moments.

That does not mean a parent, leader, grandparent, or believer must be perfect before teaching truth. If perfection were required, nobody could speak. But it does mean that the Word of God is not meant to be handed down as information only. It is meant to be lived before those who are watching.

Deuteronomy speaks of teaching diligently. Diligence is steady. It is repeated. It is not careless or occasional. It carries the sense of truth being pressed into the normal rhythms of life.

Faith is not only formed in formal settings.

It is formed at tables, in cars, before bedtime, during apologies, in moments of disappointment, in answered prayers, in hard questions, in discipline, in forgiveness, and in the way daily choices are made.

2 TIMOTHY 3:15 (ESV) speaks of the sacred writings "which are able to make you wise for salvation through faith in Christ Jesus."

That is why this matters so much. We are not simply trying to raise moral people, polite people, or religious people. We want people to know the Scriptures that point them to salvation through faith in Christ. Behaviour matters, but behaviour without the gospel cannot save.

This is not only for parents. We all influence someone. A younger believer may be watching how you walk with God. A friend may be learning from the way you respond to trial. A family member may notice whether your faith changes your speech, patience, and priorities. The people around us are often reading the truth through the consistency of our lives before they ever ask a question.

That should humble us, but it should not paralyse us.

If you have failed, repent openly where appropriate. Let those watching you see that Christianity is not pretending never to fall, but returning quickly to the Lord. Let them see the beauty of confession, forgiveness, and grace.

There is a powerful kind of teaching that happens when repentance is normal in a home. A child, a younger believer, or anyone learning from your life does not need to see a false version of strength. They need to see what it looks like when the Word of God is honoured after failure. They need to see that sin is not excused, but grace is real. They need to see that apology is not weakness and confession is not the end of hope.

PSALM 78:4 (ESV) says, "We will not hide them from their children, but tell to the coming generation the glorious deeds of the LORD." The next generation needs more than warnings about what is wrong. They need the glory of the Lord set before them. They need to hear what God has done, who He is, how He saves, why Christ is worthy, and why obedience is not empty religion but love responding to grace.

This also means the Word must not be handled as a weapon for control. EPHESIANS 6:4 (ESV) warns fathers not to provoke their children to anger, but to bring them up "in the discipline and instruction of the Lord." Instruction in the Lord should carry the character of the Lord. Truth must be firm, but it must not become harshness pretending to be holiness.

There will be questions you cannot answer perfectly. There will be moments when the conversation feels messy. There will be seasons when the fruit is not visible yet. Keep sowing the Word with patience. Keep pointing to Jesus. Keep living in a way that says the Scriptures are not only important on paper, but beautiful in life.

God's Word belongs in the everyday places of life.

Not forced in a way that becomes empty performance. Not hidden away as though faith is only private. Woven in naturally because the Lord is truly central.

Teach diligently.

Live honestly.

Point them to Christ.
