Daily Dose #172

October 13, 2026

LOOKING TO THE INTERESTS OF OTHERS

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on October 13, 2026.

PHILIPPIANS 2:3-4 (ESV)

"Do nothing from selfish ambition or conceit, but in humility count others more significant than yourselves."

The gospel challenges the way we naturally place ourselves at the centre.

Paul tells believers to do nothing from rivalry or conceit. That reaches into motives. It is possible to do the right-looking thing from the wrong inner place. We can serve because we want to be noticed. We can speak because we want to be admired. We can help because we want control. We can even appear humble while secretly wanting praise for our humility.

Scripture does not only examine what we do. It examines why we do it.

Paul then calls us to humility, saying we are to count others more significant than ourselves. This does not mean pretending everyone else is more valuable than you while you are worthless. That is not biblical humility. Every person is made in God's image. Every believer belongs to Christ by grace. Humility does not deny worth. It removes selfish superiority.

It frees us from having to be the centre.

Then Paul says we should look not only to our own things, but also to the things of others. That does not mean neglecting every responsibility or pretending your own needs do not matter. It means love breaks the grip of self-importance. We are no longer ruled by the question, "What benefits me most?"

Jesus is the pattern.

Paul goes on in Philippians 2 to point to Christ, who humbled Himself in obedience. The Son of God did not cling to status in a selfish way. He came low. He served. He obeyed the Father. He gave Himself.

MARK 10:45 (ESV) says, "For even the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve."

The Lord of glory came to serve. That truth should reshape our attitude.

Looking to the interests of others may interrupt convenience. It may mean listening when tired. It may mean changing plans. It may mean noticing someone who is often overlooked. It may mean helping without making sure everyone knows. It may mean letting another person's need matter even when it costs you comfort.

This is not always dramatic. Sometimes humility is shown in small decisions where nobody would call it ministry. Letting someone else speak. Giving attention. Being patient. Choosing not to make everything about your preference. Asking, "How can I serve here?" instead of, "Why am I not being served?"

The home needs this.

The church needs this.

Friendships need this.

Marriages, families, ministries, workplaces, and communities are all affected when people stop treating self as the only priority.

But we must be careful. Looking to the interests of others does not mean becoming controlled by people, enabling sin, or ignoring wise boundaries. Jesus served perfectly, but He was never led by people-pleasing. His love was obedient to the Father.

That is the kind of love we need. Not needy service that craves approval. Not resentful service that keeps a secret record. Not proud service that wants to be admired. Christlike service that flows from humility.

Ask where selfish ambition has been hiding under spiritual language.

Ask where conceit has made you slow to notice others.

Then look to Christ.

The One who had every right to be served came low for us. Humility begins to look different when we are standing near the cross.

1 JOHN 4:11 (ESV) says, "Beloved, if God so loved us, we also ought to love one another." The order is important. We do not love sacrificially in order to make God love us. We love because we have been loved first. The cross becomes both the fountain and the pattern.

This keeps service from becoming hollow duty. If we only look at people, we may become frustrated, because people can be difficult, ungrateful, needy, or slow to change. But if we look at Christ first, we remember how much mercy has been shown to us. We remember that love came toward us when we were not easy to love. We remember that grace was not efficient or convenient. It was costly.

Looking to the interests of others will reveal hidden selfishness, but it will also open a more joyful way to live. The self-centred life promises freedom and produces loneliness. The Christ-shaped life calls us low and fills ordinary obedience with meaning.

So ask the Lord for eyes that notice. Ask Him for a heart that serves without keeping score. Ask Him for wisdom to love truthfully, boundaries where needed, and humility where pride wants the first place.
