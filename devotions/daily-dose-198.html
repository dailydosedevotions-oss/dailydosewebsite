Daily Dose #198

November 8, 2026

Finish the Course

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on November 8, 2026.

ACTS 20:24 (ESV)

"If only I may finish my course and the ministry that I received from the Lord Jesus..."

Faithfulness is not measured only by how strongly we begin.

There is beauty in a beginning. A new step of obedience, a fresh surrender, a restored hunger for the Word, a renewed desire to serve the Lord. Beginnings matter. But Scripture also calls us to endurance.

In ACTS 20:24 (ESV), Paul says that he does not account his life of any value nor as precious to himself, if only he may finish his course and the ministry he received from the Lord Jesus. This is not carelessness with life. It is surrender to Christ above self-preservation.

Paul knew hardship was ahead. He was not walking into an easy future with romantic ideas about ministry. He understood that obedience could cost him. Yet his desire was clear: finish the course.

That phrase speaks to every believer, not only public ministers. Each follower of Christ is called to faithfulness in the place God has assigned. Your course may include hidden obedience, family responsibilities, work done unto the Lord, costly forgiveness, patient service, quiet endurance, or steadfast witness in ordinary life.

The Lord is not asking you to run someone else's race.

That truth is freeing. Comparison can exhaust the soul. We look at another person's gifts, influence, season, pace, or calling and become distracted from the obedience in front of us. But Paul speaks of the course given to him. Faithfulness begins when we stop trying to wear another person's assignment and receive our own before God.

HEBREWS 12:1 (ESV) tells believers to run "with endurance the race that is set before us." The race is set before us by the Lord. It requires endurance because weariness is real, opposition is real, and the flesh does not naturally love long obedience.

There will be days when finishing well looks quiet. Getting up again. Repenting again. Serving again. Believing again. Returning to Scripture again. Choosing truth when emotions are tired. Refusing to quit simply because obedience feels ordinary.

Paul's strength was not ambition. It was the worth of Christ and the gospel of God's grace. He wanted to testify to what he had received from the Lord Jesus.

That keeps endurance from becoming mere determination.

We do not finish the course by grit alone. We endure by grace. The One who calls His people also sustains them. The One who begins a work knows how to carry it forward.

Do not despise today's faithful step because it does not look dramatic. A course is finished by continuing.

The Lord sees the race He has set before you.

Run it with your eyes on Christ.
