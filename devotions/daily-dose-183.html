Daily Dose #183

October 24, 2026

WHEN THE SEASON CHANGES

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on October 24, 2026.

ECCLESIASTES 3:1 (ESV)

"For everything there is a season, and a time for every matter under heaven."

Seasons change, and we do not always feel ready.

Some changes bring joy. Some bring grief. Some bring both at the same time. A new season can be exciting and unsettling. It can feel like promise and loss together. It can require us to release what was familiar while trusting God for what is next.

Ecclesiastes reminds us that life under heaven moves through seasons and times. There is a time for planting and a time for uprooting, a time for mourning and a time for dancing, a time to keep and a time to cast away. Human life is not one flat experience. It moves. It shifts. It changes.

That can humble us because we are not in control of every season.

We cannot keep every door open forever. We cannot freeze every good moment. We cannot avoid every ending. We cannot force every beginning. There are times when God calls us to receive, and there are times when He calls us to release.

This does not mean every season is easy to understand.

It means God is not surprised by the turning.

DANIEL 2:21 (ESV) says, "He changes times and seasons; he removes kings and sets up kings."

God is sovereign over what feels bigger than us. The changes that unsettle us do not unsettle Him. He sees what is beginning, what is ending, what is being planted, what is being pruned, and what is being prepared.

When a season changes, we often want certainty. God may instead give us Himself. He may not reveal every detail at once, but He remains faithful in the step in front of us.

That is important because yesterday's obedience may not look exactly like today's obedience. Some rhythms may need to change. Some expectations may need surrender. Some responsibilities may need fresh grace. Some old ways of coping may no longer be able to carry the new assignment.

Ask what faithfulness looks like in this season, not the last one.

Do not despise what has ended if God used it. Do not cling to it as though God cannot be faithful again. Do not rush into the new season without Him. Do not assume uncertainty means absence.

HEBREWS 13:8 (ESV) says, "Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever."

That is the anchor. The season may change, but Christ does not become less faithful, less present, less powerful, or less kind. What steadied you before was never the season itself. It was Him.

This matters when a good season ends. Sometimes we grieve the loss of something God truly gave. Faith does not require us to call that grief wrong. But faith does refuse to make the former season our saviour. The God who met you there is able to meet you here.

It also matters when a hard season begins. ISAIAH 43:19 (ESV) says, "Behold, I am doing a new thing; now it springs forth, do you not perceive it?" God can work in ways we did not expect. A changed season is not automatically a lost season. Sometimes what feels unfamiliar is where He is preparing fresh obedience, deeper trust, and new fruit.

PSALM 31:15 (ESV) says, "My times are in your hand." That short line gives peace when timing feels outside your control. The believer's life is not held by random change. It is held by the hand of God.

Seasons change.

Christ does not.

That is where the heart can rest when life is moving.

The God who was faithful before has not changed with the season.

So do not let transition make you careless with your soul. New seasons can expose new temptations. A busy season can tempt you to neglect prayer. A waiting season can tempt you to unbelief. A painful season can tempt you to bitterness. A fruitful season can tempt you to pride. Each season needs fresh dependence on the same faithful Christ.

The question is not only, "What is changing?" The deeper question is, "How do I remain faithful while it changes?" The Lord can give wisdom for the new rhythm. He can teach you what to release without resentment and what to receive without fear.

Hold the season with open hands, but hold Christ with settled faith.

There is freedom in admitting, "Lord, I do not know this season yet, but You know me." You are not required to master every change immediately. You are called to follow the unchanging Christ faithfully through it.
