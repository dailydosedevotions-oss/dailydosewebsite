Daily Dose #151

September 22, 2026

QUIET STRENGTH

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on September 22, 2026.

ISAIAH 30:15 (ESV)

"For thus said the Lord GOD, the Holy One of Israel, 'In returning and rest you shall be saved; in quietness and in trust shall be your strength.'"

Not every strong thing is loud.

There is a kind of strength that does not need to rush, prove, force, or panic. It is not weakness. It is not denial. It is not passivity. It is the strength of a heart that has learned where safety really is.

In Isaiah 30, God's people were facing pressure, but their instinct was to look somewhere else for help. They wanted a solution that looked powerful, immediate, and visible. They wanted something they could point to and say, "This will secure us." But the Lord exposed the deeper issue. Their problem was not only political or practical. Their hearts were running from trust.

The Lord said, "In returning and rest you shall be saved; in quietness and in trust shall be your strength."

That is not how the flesh thinks strength works.

When pressure rises, we often feel that the strongest thing we can do is move faster. We try to control the conversation, control the outcome, control people's opinions, control the future, control every possibility that might go wrong. We mistake frantic movement for faithfulness because at least it feels like we are doing something.

But the Lord does not always lead His people through panic-driven activity.

Sometimes the first act of obedience is returning.

Returning means we have moved away from the place of trust. That movement may not have looked obvious to anyone else. We may still have been attending church, reading Scripture, serving, speaking Christian language, and doing the things people expect. But inwardly, the heart may have drifted. We drift from prayer into overthinking. We drift from trust into rehearsing the worst possible outcome. We drift from obedience into self-protection. We drift from listening to God into listening to fear.

The mercy of God is that He calls His people back.

He does not say, "Try harder to be your own saviour." He says, return. Come back to the place where the Lord is God and you are not. Come back to His Word. Come back to prayer. Come back to surrender. Come back to the truth that your strength was never meant to come from your ability to hold everything together.

Rest is also part of the verse. "In returning and rest you shall be saved."

Rest does not mean laziness. It does not mean refusing responsibility. It does not mean ignoring what needs to be done. Biblical rest means the soul stops trying to take God's place. It is possible to work hard while resting in God. It is also possible to sit still while inwardly striving. The issue is not only what the body is doing. It is what the heart is trusting.

Some people are exhausted not because they are doing too much with God, but because they are trying to do everything without truly trusting Him.

That kind of weariness goes deep.

It comes from carrying outcomes that belong to the Lord. It comes from trying to secure what only God can secure. It comes from believing that if we stop worrying, everything will fall apart, as though worry has been holding the world together.

But worry is not lord.

God is.

Scripture says in PSALM 46:10 (ESV), "Be still, and know that I am God."

That stillness is not empty. It is full of truth. God is God. We are not. He sees what we cannot see. He holds what we cannot control. He is not rushed, confused, surprised, or powerless. Stillness before Him is not pretending life is easy. It is refusing to let pressure take the throne.

The verse also says strength is found "in quietness and in trust."

Quietness is hard when the mind is loud. It is hard when messages need answering, bills need paying, responsibilities keep coming, and uncertainty keeps pressing. But quietness before God does not mean nothing is happening. Often, the deepest work is happening when the soul stops running long enough to remember who the Lord is.

Quietness may look like pausing before you respond. It may look like praying before making a decision. It may look like refusing to rehearse an argument in your mind all day. It may look like doing the next faithful thing without demanding that God reveal every step after it. It may look like turning off the noise so Scripture can speak louder than fear.

EXODUS 14:14 (ESV) says, "The LORD will fight for you, and you have only to be silent."

That verse was spoken to people standing in a terrifying moment, with the sea before them and Egypt behind them. Silence there was not weakness. It was trust. It was the people of God being called to stand in the truth that the Lord Himself would act.

We must be careful not to use that verse as an excuse for disobedience when God has called us to move. There are times Scripture says go forward. There are times it says speak. There are times it says work. But there are also times when the most faithful thing is to stop letting panic lead.

The world often teaches us to trust noise, speed, and control. God teaches His people to trust Him.

If your heart has been running ahead, this is an invitation to return. Not to shame. Not to defeat. To the Lord. There is strength available, but it may not come through striving harder. It may come through surrendering deeper.

The quiet place is not empty when God is there.
