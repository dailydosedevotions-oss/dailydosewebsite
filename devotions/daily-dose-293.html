Saved to the Uttermost

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on February 11, 2027.

HEBREWS 7:25 (ESV)

"He is able to save to the uttermost those who draw near to God through him."

Christ does not save halfway. HEBREWS 7:25 (ESV) says, "he is able to save to the uttermost those who draw near to God through him." That is not a fragile salvation. It is deep, complete, and held by the living intercession of Jesus.

The verse continues by saying He always lives to make intercession. The believer's hope is not in personal consistency, emotional strength, or spiritual performance. Our hope is in a Savior who lives, reigns, and intercedes.

This does not make obedience unimportant. It makes obedience possible. If salvation depended on our grip, despair would be the only honest response. But Christ holds His people with a power greater than their weakness. He saves to the uttermost, not merely to the edge of what seems manageable.

ZECHARIAH 3:4 (ESV) shows God removing filthy garments and clothing His servant with pure vestments. That picture helps us see grace: guilt removed, covering provided, and the sinner made fit by God's mercy.

If you feel beyond help, measure your need against Christ's ability, not your ability against your need. He is able to save completely. Draw near through Him. The mercy of God is not shallow, and the intercession of Christ is not weak.

This gives deep comfort for ongoing weakness. Some battles make believers wonder whether grace will run out before they are fully home. Hebrews answers by lifting our eyes to a living Savior. Jesus is not a past help only. He lives now. He intercedes now. He saves now. The Christian life rests on His continuing priestly mercy. Keep drawing near through Him, because His saving power reaches further than your worst fear.

That means today is not held together by yesterday's strength. The living Christ is sufficient for present need. Draw near again, not because you have been flawless, but because He remains able to save to the uttermost.
