Abide in Christ, Part 1: Stay Close to the Vine

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on April 8, 2027.

JOHN 15:4–5 (ESV)

“Abide in me, and I in you.”

It is possible to look busy, capable and successful while becoming empty inside. A branch can keep its shape for a while after it has been cut off, but it has already lost its source of life.

Jesus used that simple picture to explain our need for Him. He is the vine; His followers are the branches. To “abide” means to remain, stay or make your home in Him. Christianity is not merely agreeing with ideas about Jesus. It is living in daily dependence upon Him.

That dependence is not a weakness we eventually outgrow. A branch never becomes so mature that it no longer needs the vine. In the same way, spiritual maturity does not mean needing Jesus less. It means recognising more clearly that every good thing in us comes from Him.

People often try to produce the fruit of a Christian life—peace, patience, love and self-control—without remaining close to Christ. We may improve our behaviour for a time, but lasting fruit cannot be manufactured by willpower alone.

Staying close to Jesus happens through ordinary practices: listening to His words in Scripture, speaking honestly with Him in prayer, obeying what He says and returning quickly when we wander. These practices do not earn His love. They keep us near the One who has already loved us.

If faith feels dry, do not begin by asking how to look more fruitful. Begin by asking whether you are remaining close to the vine.

Stay near Jesus. Fruit follows life.
