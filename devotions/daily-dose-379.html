Words That Build, Part 1: Quick to Hear

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on May 8, 2027.

JAMES 1:19–20 (ESV)

“Let every person be quick to hear, slow to speak, slow to anger.”

Most people listen just long enough to find a place to answer.

James reverses the usual order. Be quick to hear. Slow down the words and the anger. Listening is not agreement; it is giving enough attention to understand before responding.

Anger makes us certain we already know the other person’s motives. It fills missing details with the worst interpretation and prepares a defence before the conversation has finished. James warns that human anger does not produce the righteousness God desires.

Good listening asks questions. It notices what was said and what may be underneath it. It is willing to discover that part of the problem belongs to us.

This does not mean accepting abuse or staying in an unsafe conversation. Wisdom may require stepping away. But many ordinary conflicts could change if one person stopped trying to win long enough to understand.

In your next difficult conversation, listen for the full story. Repeat what you heard before giving your reply.

Being heard can lower a wall that another argument would only strengthen.
