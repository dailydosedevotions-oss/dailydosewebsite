Daily Dose #176

October 17, 2026

THE WAY OF HUMILITY

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on October 17, 2026.

MICAH 6:8 (ESV)

"He has told you, O man, what is good; and what does the LORD require of you but to do justice, and to love kindness."

Micah gives a clear picture of a life that pleases God.

Do justice. Love kindness. Walk humbly with your God.

This is not empty religion. It is not performance without obedience. It is faith that reaches the way we live, the way we treat people, and the way we walk before the Lord.

The verse begins, "He has told you, O man, what is good." God has not left His people guessing about everything that matters. There are places where we may need wisdom for specific decisions, but His Word has already made much of His will clear. We are not free to replace obedience with religious activity.

To act justly means we care about what is right because God cares about what is right. We do not excuse dishonesty, oppression, cruelty, or partiality because it benefits us. We do not call wrong things harmless when God calls them evil.

To love kindness means we do not become hard while caring about justice. God is not calling His people to a cold righteousness that can name what is wrong while refusing compassion. The people who have received mercy should not be eager to withhold tenderness from others.

To walk humbly with God means we remember our place. We are not the source of truth. We are not the centre. We are not above correction. We walk with God, under God, before God.

These three belong together.

It is possible to care about justice without mercy and become harsh. It is possible to talk about mercy without justice and become careless about truth. It is possible to do outwardly good things while walking in pride. Scripture refuses to let us separate what God has joined.

ISAIAH 66:2 (ESV) says, "But this is the one to whom I will look: he who is humble and contrite in spirit."

God looks with favour on the humble, contrite person who trembles at His Word. That is very different from religious pride. Religious pride wants to look impressive. Humility wants to obey.

Jesus confronted religious people who honoured God with lips while their hearts were far from Him. That warning still matters. We can speak about justice, mercy, and humility while living in ways that contradict them. We can use the right language and still avoid the surrender God requires.

Micah's words bring us back to what is good.

Do what is right.

Love what reflects the kindness of God.

Walk low before God.

This will reach into ordinary moments. It may shape how you handle money, how you speak about others, how you respond to someone who has failed, how you treat someone with less influence, how quickly you repent, and how willing you are to be corrected by Scripture.

JAMES 1:27 (ESV) says that religion that is pure and undefiled before God includes caring for orphans and widows in their affliction and keeping oneself unstained from the world. Again, Scripture holds together what we often separate: compassion toward others and holiness before God.

That means humility is not only an inward feeling. It has a direction. It bends toward obedience. It refuses to use worship language as a covering for selfish living. It refuses to call itself spiritual while ignoring the vulnerable, excusing sin, or resisting correction.

The Lord is not impressed by religious activity that avoids surrender. In Micah's day, people could bring offerings while missing the heart of what God required. In our day, we can attend, post, speak, sing, serve, and still avoid the plain commands of Scripture. That should make us sober.

But it should also give clarity. You do not have to invent a complicated version of faithfulness today. Do what is right before God. Love kindness because you have received mercy. Walk humbly because every breath, every gift, every open door, and every forgiveness you have received is grace.

The way of humility is not vague.

The Lord has spoken clearly enough for today's obedience. We may not know every detail about tomorrow, but we know enough to do justice, love kindness, and walk humbly with God where our feet are now.

It is walked one obedient step at a time.

Humility keeps returning to the plain things God has already said. It refuses to hide behind complexity when obedience is clear. The Lord is honoured not only by the songs we sing, but by the justice, kindness, and lowliness that mark the life after the song is over.

Walk humbly enough to obey the simple word.
