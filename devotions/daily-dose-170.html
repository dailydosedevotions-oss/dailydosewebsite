Daily Dose #170

October 11, 2026

CARRY ONE ANOTHER

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on October 11, 2026.

GALATIANS 6:2 (ESV)

"Bear one another's burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ."

Faith was never meant to make us indifferent to the weight others carry.

Paul tells believers to bear one another's burdens. This is not intrusion. It is not control. It is not taking the place of God in someone else's life. It is love that comes close enough to help.

Some burdens are too heavy for a person to carry alone. Grief can become heavy. Temptation can become heavy. Weakness, pressure, confusion, family pain, weariness, and suffering can become crushing in isolation. The church is called to be a people who do not simply notice weight from a distance, but help carry it with humility and love.

This requires attention.

People do not always announce their burdens clearly. Sometimes heaviness hides behind normal routines. Sometimes the person who keeps showing up is quietly tired. Sometimes the one who encourages everyone else also needs encouragement. Sometimes a burden is visible, but everyone assumes someone else will help.

Bearing burdens means love refuses to be careless.

ROMANS 12:15 (ESV) says, "Rejoice with those who rejoice, weep with those who weep." Love enters both joy and sorrow. It does not only appear when the moment is convenient. It learns how to celebrate without jealousy and grieve without rushing people through pain.

But bearing burdens also requires humility on both sides.

It takes humility to help without pride. We are not saviours. We are servants. We do not carry someone else's burden so we can feel important, needed, or superior. We help because Christ has loved us.

It also takes humility to receive help. Some people are crushed because pride keeps them silent. They do not want to appear weak. They do not want anyone to know the weight is heavy. But God often strengthens His people through His people.

1 CORINTHIANS 12:26 (ESV) says, "If one member suffers, all suffer together."

That is the body of Christ. We are connected. Another person's suffering is not irrelevant. Another person's restoration should not feel like competition. Love refuses isolation and comparison.

There are also boundaries in burden bearing. Carrying one another does not mean enabling sin, taking responsibility for what someone refuses to surrender, or becoming the centre of another person's life. Biblical love helps in truth. It points to Christ. It serves without becoming lord.

Ask God to open your eyes.

Who near you is carrying more than they are saying? Who needs prayer, encouragement, patience, practical help, wise counsel, or steady presence? And where have you been too proud to let someone else help you?

Jesus shows us what this compassion looks like. MATTHEW 9:36 (ESV) says, "When he saw the crowds, he had compassion for them, because they were harassed and helpless, like sheep without a shepherd." He saw more than a crowd. He saw the condition of people. He saw need beneath the surface.

We need that kind of sight. Not suspicious curiosity. Not a desire to know everyone's business. Compassion that notices enough to love wisely. Sometimes burden bearing is practical. A visit. A meal. A message. A lift. A shared responsibility. Sometimes it is spiritual. Prayer, Scripture, gentle correction, encouragement to keep going. Sometimes it is simply staying present when there is no quick answer to give.

There is also a burden-bearing that protects truth. GALATIANS 6:1 (ESV) says that if anyone is caught in transgression, those who are spiritual should restore him "in a spirit of gentleness." Restoration is part of love. We do not help people by ignoring sin that is crushing them. But even correction must be carried gently, remembering our own weakness.

The law of Christ is not fulfilled by distance, pride, or cold advice. It is fulfilled when the love of Jesus moves through His people in patient, truthful, practical care.

The law of Christ is fulfilled through love that comes close.

Someone near you may not need a perfect answer as much as a faithful presence. Someone may need you to pray, listen, help, or simply refuse to let them suffer alone. Burden bearing is often ordinary, but in the hands of Christ, ordinary love becomes holy service.

Do not step over the burden because it is not yours.

Christ carried what we could never carry for ourselves. That does not make us saviours for one another, but it does make us servants. The mercy we have received should make us slower to pass by suffering and quicker to ask how love can become practical.

Sometimes the smallest faithful help becomes the hand of God to someone who felt alone.
