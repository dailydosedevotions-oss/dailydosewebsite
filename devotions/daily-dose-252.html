First Things First: Part 1

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on January 1, 2027.

PSALM 5:3 (ESV)

"O LORD, in the morning you hear my voice; in the morning I prepare a sacrifice for you and watch."

The first day of a year can make people think about change, goals, plans, and hopes. Those things can have their place, but Scripture leads us deeper than ambition. David says in PSALM 5:3 (ESV), "O LORD, in the morning you hear my voice." Before the day is filled with demands, David turns toward the Lord. Before the answers are visible, he prepares his heart and watches.

This is not about pretending every morning feels peaceful. Some mornings begin with tiredness, pressure, or the weight of what has carried over from yesterday. But the pattern matters: the Lord is not an afterthought. The day is brought before Him. The heart speaks to Him before it is swallowed by noise. A year rightly begun is not merely a year with better organisation; it is a life increasingly surrendered to God.

David says he prepares and watches. That is faith with expectancy. He brings himself before the Lord and waits under God's authority. This is different from rushing into the day already ruled by fear, messages, tasks, and self-reliance. PSALM 143:8 (ESV) says, "Let me hear in the morning of your steadfast love, for in you I trust." Morning trust does not guarantee an easy day, but it puts the heart in the right place before the true King.

So begin here. Not with pressure to become impressive. Not with promises you may not keep in your own strength. Begin with the Lord. Bring Him the year, the day, the unknown, the hopes, the weaknesses, and the places where you need mercy. First things first: let your voice rise to Him, and let your heart watch for His faithful hand.

This opening also protects the year from being built on pressure. There is a difference between disciplined planning and anxious self-dependence. The believer can plan, but plans must be placed before God with open hands. Beginning with the Lord reminds us that the year is not ours to control, perform, or carry alone. It is lived before Him, one day at a time, with mercy for today's obedience and trust for what has not yet been revealed.
