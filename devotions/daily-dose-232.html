A Life of Worshipful Waiting

This devotion is scheduled and will become available at 7am on December 12, 2026.

LUKE 2:36-38 (ESV)

"She began to give thanks to God."

Anna's life reminds us that hidden devotion is not forgotten by God. Luke tells us she was advanced in years, a widow, and deeply devoted. She did not depart from the temple, worshiping with fasting and prayer night and day. Her life may not have looked impressive to the world, but Scripture records it with honour. When she sees the child Jesus, LUKE 2:38 (ESV) says she begins to give thanks to God and speak of Him to all who were waiting for the redemption of Jerusalem. Her waiting becomes witness. That is beautiful. Anna had known loss. She had known years. She had known worship when many ordinary days may have looked the same. Yet when Christ is revealed, she responds with thanksgiving and testimony. Long faithfulness matters.

Some obedience is not dramatic. It is daily. Worship again. Seek God again. Stay faithful again. Pray again. Give thanks again. Speak truth again. The world may not clap for that kind of life, but heaven is not blind to it. MATTHEW 6:4 (ESV) speaks of the Father "who sees in secret." That does not mean we earn His favour by our devotion. It means the Lord sees what is done unto Him, even when no one else notices. Anna also shows that age and suffering do not make someone useless in the kingdom of God. Her testimony still mattered. Her worship still mattered. Her voice still pointed people to redemption. Do not despise hidden years. Do not assume worship is wasted because it is quiet. Do not think waiting has no purpose when Christ is the One you are waiting for. Anna saw Him and gave thanks. A life that waits with worship is a life ready to recognize mercy when it comes. That kind of life is not formed in a hurry. It is shaped through repeated surrender, ordinary faithfulness, and worship when no one is making much of it. PSALM 92:14 (ESV) says of the righteous, "They still bear fruit in old age." Age, loss, and long waiting do not make a person useless to God. A heart that remains before Him can still bear witness, still give thanks, and still point others toward redemption.
